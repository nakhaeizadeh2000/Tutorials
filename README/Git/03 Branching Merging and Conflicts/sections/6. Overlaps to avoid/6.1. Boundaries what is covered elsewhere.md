## 6.1. Boundaries: what is covered elsewhere

### 1) **Label theory and remotes**

The branch-as-pointer model — labels on commits, merges as two-parent joins — belongs to [Domain 01 1.2. The commit DAG](<../../../01 Git Fundamentals and Mental Model/sections/1. Thinking in snapshots/1.2. The commit DAG history as a graph.md>); this domain assumed it and taught the *operations*. Everything across the network — tracking branches, `fetch`/`pull`/`push`, forks, PR flow, keeping a fork current — belongs to Domain 04 (planned, see LOG). Local `-d` never touches the remote (leaf 1.1); `push --delete` plus `fetch --prune` is the remote half, and it lives there.

- **Boundary rule**: if the sentence moves `HEAD` on your machine, it lives here. If it moves refs on someone else's machine, it lives in Domain 04. The local/remote split is the handshake between the two — defined once, referenced from both sides.

### 2) **Rebase, cherry-pick, and history surgery**

Replaying commits onto new bases instead of joining lines, plucking single commits across lines, rewriting shared history and its golden rule, amending messages, `reflog` recovery — all of it belongs to Domain 05 (planned, see LOG), not here. This domain merges lines; Domain 05 rewrites them. The replay-vs-join decision (`rebase` vs `merge`, and why shared lines must never be rewritten) is the most consequential choice in local Git — it gets its full treatment exactly once, there.

- **Boundary rule**: joining preserves both lines' history (this domain); replaying rewrites one line's history onto another (Domain 05). If the command changes existing commit hashes, it is surgery — link forward, never explain here.

### 3) **Branching *strategy***

Trunk-based vs GitFlow shapes, release lines, which lines are long-lived and who may merge into them, protected branches, merge queues — *policy* belongs to Domain 06 (planned, see LOG); this domain owns *mechanics*. Short-lived feature branches (leaf 1.2) are the mechanic every strategy shares; which long-lived lines they target is the strategy's decision.

- **Boundary rule**: mechanics ("how do I branch, join, resolve?") live here; policy ("which lines exist, who merges where, when do we cut?") lives in Domain 06. If the sentence names a branch that outlives a sprint, it is policy — link forward.

### 4) **Common confusion**

- "Branching is done after this domain" — local branching mechanics are done; remote collaboration (04), rescue (05), and policy (06) build on them. Fluency here is the prerequisite, not the finish line.
- "Overlaps mean duplication" — no: every cross-boundary mention in this track is one line plus a link (DRY single-source-of-truth). Full treatment lives in exactly one place.
- "This boundary list will go stale" — it is maintained: each Domain 04–07 session upgrades the corresponding line here from "(planned, see LOG)" to a live link, continuing the placeholder-retirement discipline from Sessions 1–2.

### 5) **Mentor note**

Teach boundaries as a map, consistent with Domains 01–02: "model was east (01), fluency was practice (02), branching is daily teamwork (03), the network is next (04), rescue is the hospital (05), policy is the constitution (06)." Learners who can place any Git question into exactly one domain bucket have internalized the track's architecture — and they stop demanding rebase answers from branching material. Review check for this domain: the learner places "should we rebase the feature?", "who approves merges to release?", and "how do I un-merge Thursday?" into 05, 06, and 05 respectively — placement accuracy *is* branching mastery.
