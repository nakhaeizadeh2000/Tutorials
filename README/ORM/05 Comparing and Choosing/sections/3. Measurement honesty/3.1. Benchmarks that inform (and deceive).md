# 3.1. Benchmarks that inform (and deceive)

## 1. Measure your workload (your queries, your data, your concurrency — vendor benchmarks never)

**Definition** — the measurement rule: performance evidence comes from *your* workload (your queries (hot paths listed — top-10 by frequency × cost); your data (production-scale volumes and distributions — celebrities included ([Database Design §4.3](<../../../../DatabaseDesign/04 Relationships at Scale/sections/3. Skew and hot relationships/3.1. Hot keys and skew.md>) — means *and* maximums); your concurrency (connection counts, worker parallelism, peak multipliers — load shaped like production, not like localhost-idle). Vendor benchmarks (tool authors' hello-world shootouts — unrepresentative by construction — next promise dissects) inform never (different queries, different data, different concurrency — numbers inapplicable by design).

**What problem it solves** — the benchmark-driven selection duo: *vendor-benchmark adoption* ("X is 3× faster" (hello-world CRUD on localhost, single connection, empty caches — production resembling it nowhere (concurrent load untested, N+1 uncounted, transfer unmeasured — numbers inapplicable, decisions misled); and *no-measurement adoption* (tools chosen on DX alone (ergonomics evaluated — performance hoped ("should scale fine" — adjectives, not arithmetic — linear latencies discovering scale, incidents scheduling). Own-workload measurement converts both (hot paths listed, production volumes seeded, concurrency simulated — numbers applicable (same queries/data/concurrency as production — decisions evidence-backed, surprises preempted).

**Modern guidance** — the measurement lines per selection (review-enforced): *workload listed* (hot paths enumerated (top-10 by frequency × cost — from access logs/analytics, not imagination — unlisted paths unmeasured (blind spots scheduled); *data seeded* (production-scale volumes AND distributions (celebrity rows included — maximums gauged, not just means ([Database Design §4.3](<../../../../DatabaseDesign/04 Relationships at Scale/sections/3. Skew and hot relationships/3.1. Hot keys and skew.md>) — staging tens hiding production ten-thousands is measurement malpractice); *concurrency simulated* (connection counts, worker parallelism, peak multipliers (load shaped like production Friday-evenings, not Tuesday-3am-idle); *candidates compared* (same workload, candidate tools, statement counts + latencies (verified live pattern from [1.1](<../1. Selection by workload/1.1. Workload-matched selection.md>): raw-loop 6, raw-join 1, drizzle-with 1, prisma-include 2, typeorm-relations 1 — counts compared, not milliseconds worshipped). Missing line → request changes (unmeasured selection failing structurally — numbers or it didn't happen).

```typescript
// GOOD: workload measured — hot paths, production data, simulated concurrency (excerpt).
// Workload: order-list (800k/day) + history (50k/day) + dashboard rollup (1k/day) — top-3 listed.
// Data: production-scale seed (10M orders, flagship groups included — maximums gauged).
// Concurrency: 200 connections, 16 workers, 3× peak multiplier (Friday-evening shaped).
// Candidates: raw-join 1 stmt, drizzle-with 1, prisma-include 2, typeorm-relations 1
// (verified live pattern [1.1] — counts compared; latencies measured per candidate at concurrency).
// Verdict: evidence-backed (same workload/data/concurrency — numbers applicable).
```

```typescript
// BAD: vendor-benchmarked or unmeasured — numbers inapplicable or absent (real failure shapes).
// "X benchmarks 3× faster (vendor hello-world: single query, localhost, idle — production resembling
// it nowhere (concurrent load untested (200 connections unmodeled); N+1 uncounted (loops unbenched);
// transfer unmeasured (SELECT-* unexamined). Or: "should scale fine" (adjectives, not arithmetic —
// linear latencies discovering scale (incidents scheduling). Benchmarks inapplicable; adjectives
// unmeasured. Decisions misled or unmade — both billed quarterly.)
```

**Common confusion** — *"our scale is small (measurement overkill)."* Small-scale measurement costs minutes (hot paths listed from logs (access logs exist at every scale); staging seeded (fixtures sized sanely — hundreds, not tens); concurrency eyeballed (peak multipliers estimated — margins named). Unmeasured small scale grows into unmeasured large scale (assumptions fossilizing (tools chosen on vibes — misfits compounding with growth — migration quarters later for measurement minutes now). "Small" describes current load (measurement cheap — minutes); growth describes future load (unmeasured decisions billing quarterly). Measure small (minutes); scale measured (decisions aging well); never skip (size-independent habit — measurers at every scale).

**Performance note** — measurement economics: workload listing costs log queries (minutes — hot paths enumerated); seeding costs fixture work (production-scale volumes + celebrities — hours once, reused per comparison); concurrency simulation costs load-tooling (k6/vegeta scripts — hours, reusable); unmeasured equivalents cost misfit quarters (wrong-tool incidents (linear latencies, ceremony drag — remediation quarters); vendor-misled quarters (hello-world numbers meeting production reality — surprises scheduled). Minutes + hours prevent quarters — measurement ROI exceeding all other selection investments (evidence-backed decisions aging well; fashion/vibes decaying immediately).

**Mentor note** — teach "measure your workload" (juniors listing hot paths + seeding production-scale + simulating concurrency design evidence-backed selections on first hearing; vendor-quoting interrogated ("their queries or ours?" — applicability demanded per number). Benchmark literacy as hiring signal (candidates dissecting methodologies (next promise) outscore candidates reciting numbers — method over metrics, always). Measure workloads; dissect benchmarks; decide evidenced.

## 2. Count statements first (N+1 dominates all micro-differences — batching decided before benchmarking)

**Definition** — the ordering rule: statement counts precede latency benchmarks (N+1 audited per list (loop 3 vs batched 1 — verified live pattern [1.1](<../1. Selection by workload/1.1. Workload-matched selection.md>) — linearities eliminated before microseconds compared); batching decided (includes/relations/with: per path — constant statements regardless of N — verified across all three tools S2–S4); micro-differences benchmarked last (mapper overhead per row (microseconds — measurable, negligible beside I/O — benchmarked only after counts bounded (unbounded counts dwarfing micro-differences by orders of magnitude — benchmarking unbatched paths measures N+1 speed, not tool speed).

**What problem it solves** — the micro-benchmark trap (hello-world CRUD timed per tool (Prisma 1.2ms vs Drizzle 0.9ms vs raw 0.7ms — microseconds differing (I/O-dominated production rendering differences invisible — decisions made on noise); N+1 uncounted (loops unbenched (benchmarks fetching single rows — list shapes untested — linear latencies invisible in single-row numbers); batching undecided (strategies unstated — benchmarked defaults differing arbitrarily (eager vs lazy vs manual — incomparable configurations compared as tool properties). Counts-first converts micro-noise to structural signal (statements bounded (N+1 eliminated — batching decided); micro-differences benchmarked after (mapper overhead per row — real but negligible — decisions informed, never driven).

**Modern guidance** — the ordering lines per comparison (review-enforced): *count* (statements per list per tool (verified live pattern: raw-loop 6, raw-join 1, drizzle-with 1, prisma-include 2, typeorm-relations 1 — linearities exposed (loops) vs constants (batches) — batching decided before timing anything); *batch* (strategies stated per relation per tool (includes/relations/with: — unbatched paths fixed (loops rewritten — counts re-verified (constant confirmed); *micro-benchmark* (mapper overhead per row *after* counts bounded (microseconds measured — I/O-dominated production rendering differences moot — numbers recorded for completeness, decided never). Missing counts → request changes (micro-benchmarks without statement bounds measuring N+1 speed — benchmarking the wrong thing quickly). Counts first; batching second; microscopes last.

```typescript
// GOOD: counts first — linearities eliminated, micro-differences contextualized (verified live numbers).
// Counts: raw-loop 6, raw-join 1, drizzle-with 1, prisma-include 2, typeorm-relations 1 (verified [1.1]).
// Batching: loops rewritten (includes/with:/relations — constant statements — verified by recount).
// Micro: mapper overhead per row (microseconds — measured post-batching — I/O-dominated (negligible).
// Verdict: tools separated by statements (structural — batching decided), not microseconds (noise).
```

```typescript
// BAD: microscopes first — micro-differences worshipped, linearities uncounted (real shapes).
// "Prisma 1.2ms vs Drizzle 0.9ms vs raw 0.7ms" (single-row hello-world (N+1 untested — loops unbenched;
// batching undecided (strategies unstated — incomparable configurations compared as tool properties);
// transfer unmeasured (SELECT-* unexamined). Microseconds differing (I/O-dominated production rendering
// differences invisible — noise worshipped); linearities uncounted (N+1 scheduled — quarters billing).
// Noise deciding; signal uncounted. Microscope first, telescope never.)
```

**Common confusion** — *"faster hello-world means faster production (extrapolation valid)."* Extrapolation requires *representativeness* (benchmark workload ≈ production workload (queries/data/concurrency matching — hello-world matching none (single rows vs lists (N+1 absent); idle vs concurrent (contention absent); empty vs production-scale data (transfer/index-depth absent). Unrepresentative numbers extrapolate to fiction (fast hello-world, slow production lists — surprise scheduled (linearities uncounted — micro-wins irrelevant). Representativeness first (own workload benched); extrapolation second (numbers applicable); hello-world never (unrepresentative by construction — next promise dissects fully).

**Performance note** — ordering economics: counting costs test wrappers (verified live pattern — minutes, once); batching costs strategy decisions (minutes per relation — N+1 eliminated structurally); micro-benchmarking costs harness hours (load tooling, statistical rigor — confidence intervals, warmups — real benchmarking expensive); uncounted micro-worship costs linear incidents (N+1 at scale — quarters each — micro-wins irrelevant beside linearities). Counts cheap (minutes); batching structural (decisions); microscopes expensive (hours for noise). Order: counts, batching, microscopes — economics dictating sequence, permanently.

**Mentor note** — teach "counts before clocks" (juniors counting statements before timing queries design measurement discipline on first hearing; clock-first interrogated ("counted?" — measurement order demanded per benchmark). Demonstrate micro-irrelevance once (1.2ms vs 0.9ms single-row delta beside 10k-statement N+1 (microseconds vs quarters — perspective set permanently — clocks humbled, counts crowned). Count first; batch second; clock last — measurement order in three habits, reviewed per benchmark.

## 3. Micro-benchmarks deceive systematically (hello-world shootouts — unrepresentative by construction)

**Definition** — the dissection skill: vendor/community ORM shootouts audited for representativeness (workload match? (hello-world CRUD vs production lists/graphs — mismatch disqualifying); N+1 counted? (single-row fetches vs list shapes — linearities untested — numbers silent where production spends); concurrency modeled? (idle vs loaded (contention absent — pool exhaustion, lock waits, replica lag — untested); transfer measured? (SELECT-* unexamined (bytes unbenched — transfer dominating real latencies); statistics rigorous? (warmups? intervals? runs? — single-shot numbers (noise presented as signal — confidence absent). Unrepresentative on any axis → number inapplicable (regardless of magnitude — 10× on hello-world predicts nothing about production lists).

**What problem it solves** — the shootout-driven selection (tool chosen on benchmark charts (vendor-published (conflicted — authors benchmarking their own tool (configurations tuned asymmetrically — competitors defaulted, house optimized); community-published (well-meaning, unrepresentative (hello-world shapes — single rows, idle concurrency, empty data — production resembling nowhere); Hacker-News-upvoted (popularity mistaken for methodology — votes measuring enthusiasm, not rigor). Selections on shootouts inherit unrepresentativeness (decisions optimized for workloads never run — production surprises scheduled (linearities uncounted (N+1 absent from charts); concurrency unmodeled (contention absent); transfer unmeasured (bytes absent). Dissection converts charts to questions (representative of what? — answered per axis — inapplicable dismissed, applicable rare).

**Modern guidance** — the dissection lines per benchmark (review-enforced): *workload* (queries matching production hot paths? (lists/graphs/dynamics — hello-world CRUD disqualifying (unrepresentative — dismissed regardless of margin); *N+1* (list shapes benched? (statement counts published? — single-row numbers silent on linearities — uncounted benchmarks inapplicable to list-heavy production); *concurrency* (load modeled? (connections/workers/peaks — idle numbers predicting loaded behavior never (contention unmodeled — pool/lock/replica realities absent); *transfer* (bytes measured? (SELECT-* unexamined — transfer dominating real latencies (bandwidth billed per render — unbenched); *statistics* (warmups/intervals/runs reported? (single-shot numbers — noise presented — confidence absent (error bars missing — margins meaningless). Failing any axis → inapplicable (dismissed politely, decided independently via own-workload measurement (promise 1)).

```typescript
// GOOD: benchmark dissected — axes checked, applicability judged (excerpt).
// Chart: "X 3× faster" (hello-world single-row CRUD, idle, empty DB).
// Workload: lists/graphs absent (production mismatched — disqualifying).
// N+1: uncounted (single rows only — linearities silent — inapplicable to list-heavy prod).
// Concurrency: idle (200-connection reality unmodeled — contention absent).
// Transfer: unmeasured (SELECT-* unexamined — bytes absent where latencies live).
// Statistics: single-shot (noise presented — confidence absent).
// Verdict: inapplicable (dismissed — own-workload measurement commissioned instead).
```

```typescript
// BAD: chart-driven selection — numbers worshipped, methodology unexamined (real shapes).
// "X benchmarks fastest — adopting." (Workload? Unchecked (hello-world vs production lists —
// mismatch unnoticed). N+1? Uncounted (single-row numbers — linearities silent). Concurrency?
// Idle (loaded reality unmodeled). Transfer? Unmeasured (bytes absent). Statistics? Single-shot
// (noise worshipped). Chart admired; methodology unexamined; production surprised (linearities
// uncounted (N+1 scheduled); contention unmodeled (pools exhausting); transfer unmeasured (bytes
// billing). Worshipped numbers; scheduled surprises.)
```

**Common confusion** — *"all benchmarks are useless (dismiss measurement entirely)."* Own-workload benchmarks invaluable (hot paths listed, production data seeded, concurrency simulated — numbers applicable (same queries/data/concurrency — decisions evidence-backed); vendor shootouts inapplicable (unrepresentative axes — dismissed per dissection, not per cynicism). "Useless" confuses unrepresentative benchmarks (dismissed methodologically — axes checked, applicability judged) with measurement itself (own-workload numbers — gold standard, always commissioned). Dismiss shootouts (dissected); commission measurements (workload-matched); confuse never (cynicism and credulity both failing — methodology distinguishing).

**Performance note** — dissection economics: auditing a benchmark costs minutes (five axes checked — workload/N+1/concurrency/transfer/statistics — inapplicability determined quickly); commissioning own-workload measurement costs hours (listing/seeding/simulating/comparing — evidence-backed decisions aging well); chart-driven misselection costs quarters (wrong-tool incidents (linear latencies, ceremony drag — remediation quarters); re-migration (tool switches post-discovery — rewrite quarters). Minutes dissecting + hours measuring prevent quarters misselecting — methodology ROI exceeding all other selection investments (evidence over enthusiasm, permanently).

**Mentor note** — teach "dissect, don't dismiss-or-worship" (juniors auditing benchmark methodology design critical judgment on first hearing; worship interrogated ("representative of what?" — applicability demanded per number); cynicism corrected ("commission own-workload instead" — dismissal paired with alternative — critique constructive, never nihilistic). Benchmark literacy as hiring signal (candidates dissecting methodologies outscoring number-reciters — method over metrics, always — same verdict as workload measurement (promise 1)). Dissect methodically; commission independently; decide evidenced.
