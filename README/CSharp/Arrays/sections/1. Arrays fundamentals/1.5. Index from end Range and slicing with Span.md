## 1.5. Index-from-end (`^`), `Range` (`..`), and slicing with `Span<T>` / `ReadOnlySpan<T>`

### APIs

1. **`System.Index`**: `^1` means “one from the end” (not a negative index).
2. **`System.Range`**: `start..end` uses **half-open** interval rules (`..^0` reaches the end).
3. **Slicing**: `array[x..y]`, `span[x..y]` produce **views** (spans) without copying when using span APIs.

### Arrays (`T[]`)

1. `arr[^1]` is readable and bounds-checked.
2. **Allocations**: `arr[..]` on arrays may allocate a **new array** in some APIs—know what overload you call.

### Spans (performance default for hot paths)

1. **`ReadOnlySpan<T>`**: non-allocating slice of contiguous memory.
2. **Prefer spans** for parsing, UTF-8 text, and tight loops.
3. **Lifetime**: spans can’t escape beyond their backing storage rules—async/iterator restrictions apply.

### Collections

1. Types with **span-friendly** surface area integrate best with modern APIs.
2. For `List<T>`, use **`CollectionsMarshal.AsSpan`** only with extreme care (mutable backing array; advanced).
