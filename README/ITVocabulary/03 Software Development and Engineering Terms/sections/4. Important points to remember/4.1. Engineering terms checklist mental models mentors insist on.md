## 4.1. Engineering terms checklist (mental models mentors insist on)

### 1) **Name the change unit**

Commit, PR, or release — in every status, every standup, every incident update (from [1.1](<../1. Change words/1.1. Repo branch commit and merge.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "PR #412 (3 commits, feat/coupon-discount) awaiting second review" — not "my change is almost done". The unit noun tells listeners where to look and what state it is in.
- **Bad habit to break**: file-talk ("I edited checkout.js") — files are state, changes are history; teams coordinate on history.

### 2) **Name the gate**

Which test, whose approval, which environment — before anything merges or ships (from [1.2](<../1. Change words/1.2. Pull request review and conflicts.md>), [2.1](<../2. Ship words/2.1. Build test release and deploy.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "unit + integration green, approved by Sara, staging verified" — three gates, three facts. Any merge missing one is a risk someone accepted blind.
- **Bad habit to break**: force-merging past red ("tests are flaky, ship it") — normalizing broken gates converts the pipeline from protection into decoration.

### 3) **Name the debt**

What shortcut, what interest, which ticket — at the moment of taking it (from [3.1](<../3. Health words/3.1. Refactor tech debt legacy and cleanup.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "shortcut: hardcoded rate limit (DEBT-44, ~1 day interest per pricing change)" — priced, tracked, plannable. Unnamed debt is the kind that halves velocity by surprise.
- **Bad habit to break**: silent shortcuts discovered quarters later as "the code sucks" — archaeology nobody budgeted for.

### 4) **Common confusion**

- "Process words are bureaucracy" — naming units, gates, and debt is the minimum coordination protocol that lets strangers collaborate safely; bureaucracy is process without purpose, this is the opposite.
- "Checklists replace judgment" — they *stage* judgment: mechanics checked mechanically, brains reserved for the genuinely novel call.

### 5) **Mentor note**

Put the triple-name rule in the team's PR template: change-unit line, gates line, debt line (or "no debt taken"). Templates beat lectures — every author practices the vocabulary on every change, and reviewers get the facts in fixed positions instead of excavating them from prose.
