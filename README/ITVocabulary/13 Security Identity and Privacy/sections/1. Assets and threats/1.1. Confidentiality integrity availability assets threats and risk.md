## 1.1. Confidentiality integrity availability assets threats and risk

A service can keep its participant list secret while publishing the wrong session
time. It can preserve both correctly while remaining unavailable when staff need
it. “Secure” needs more detail than one successful protection or one reassuring
product label. Begin with what matters, what could happen to it and the consequences.

### 1) **Name the asset and the security property that matters**

An **asset** is something of value within the situation being assessed. Data,
services, credentials, devices and the ability to carry out a business activity
can matter. Name what is valuable and to whom. “The database” may be too broad
when one discussion concerns private participant details and another concerns
accurate public session times.

The **CIA triad** groups three common information-security objectives:

| Objective | Practical meaning | Constructed workshop example |
|---|---|---|
| Confidentiality | Preserve authorized restrictions on access and disclosure | Participant contact details are available only to authorized staff |
| Integrity | Guard against improper modification or destruction | Published session times reflect the authorized schedule |
| Availability | Provide timely, reliable access and use | Staff can retrieve the roster when checking people in |

These meanings follow the security-objective discussion in
[NIST FIPS 199](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.199.pdf), published
in February 2004. It is a scoped federal categorization standard, not a new
runtime release or a declaration that its compliance process applies to every
project. Its example of public information also illustrates why information can
need integrity and availability even when confidentiality is not the primary goal.

An authorized schedule change does not violate integrity merely because bytes
changed. Conversely, an accidental unauthorized overwrite can damage integrity
without a malicious attacker. These objectives describe properties to preserve;
they do not select a mechanism or explain the cause of every observed failure.
They are a useful starting model, not an exhaustive account of privacy, safety
or all organizational concerns.

Keep overloaded terms scoped. Database **ACID consistency** concerns defined
invariants; it is not the same label as confidentiality or a complete security
assessment. **CAP availability** has a particular distributed-system model and
response condition. A claim about one does not automatically satisfy the service's
user-facing availability needs.

### 2) **Separate a possible harmful event from its risk assessment**

A **threat** is a circumstance or event with the potential for harm. A **threat
source** can be an adversary, a human mistake, a component failure or a wider
physical event. A **threat actor** refers to an acting person or group in the
adversarial discussion; a power failure is not an actor. A **threat event** describes
what could happen. “An outsider obtains the roster through an unintended public
export” is more useful than merely naming “hackers.”

A **vulnerability** is a weakness relevant to such harm; the next lesson distinguishes
weaknesses, attacks and incidents more fully. Do not equate the threat with the
weakness, or a possible threat with evidence that an attack occurred.

**Risk** considers potential harm in context, including its likelihood and impact.
**Impact** concerns the consequence; **likelihood** concerns the chance of the
specified event and resulting harm within a stated situation and time frame.
**Risk assessment** identifies, analyzes and prioritizes such risks using an explicit
model and assumptions. NIST's [SP 800-30 revision 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-30r1.pdf)
provides this distinction and includes adversarial and non-adversarial sources.
It does not require one universal arithmetic formula for combining every factor.

A spreadsheet score such as likelihood rating times impact rating is meaningful
only within its declared scale and method. An ordinal rating of 4 is not automatically
a 40% probability, and a score of 12 is not a measured expected financial loss.
Ask for the event, affected asset, period, evidence, existing protections and
uncertainty before comparing scores from different teams.

**Threat modeling** examines a representation of a system to identify concerns
and possible responses. The practitioners' original
[Threat Modeling Manifesto](https://www.threatmodelingmanifesto.org/)
connects understanding the system, identifying what could go wrong, choosing
responses and evaluating them. It presents adaptable principles rather than one
required diagram or a certificate of security. A useful model helps people make
specific decisions; merely listing famous attacks does not establish relevance.

### 3) **Read a security claim through a concrete harm scenario**

Use this **fictional assessment packet**. It is a vocabulary exercise, not a
security test or professional risk assessment of a real organization.

| Packet fact | Meaning to retain |
|---|---|
| Public catalog contains session titles and authorized start times | The catalog is intentionally public; schedule correctness still matters |
| Staff roster contains participant names and contact details | Authorized access restrictions are relevant to this separate dataset |
| A transport check confirms TLS on browser-to-service traffic | Evidence about that connection; storage and export permissions are not described |
| The team proposes a public CSV link containing the roster | A proposed disclosure path to review; no observed access is supplied |
| Check-in requires roster access during a stated morning window | Availability has a user task and time context |
| No likelihood analysis, export-access check or recovery evidence supplied | Those questions remain unanswered in this packet |

A worked response is:

> TLS evidence covers one connection. It does not establish that a public export
> preserves roster confidentiality. Identify who can retrieve the proposed file
> and whether that access is authorized. The public catalog still needs protection
> against improper schedule changes, and staff need roster access during check-in.
> The packet identifies distinct harm scenarios but supplies no basis for a
> numerical risk ranking or a claim that an unauthorized disclosure already occurred.

Now change only the proposed CSV contents to public session titles and times,
with participant details absent. That removes the described roster-disclosure
content from this proposed file. It does not establish that all other roster
paths are protected, or that the catalog's integrity and check-in availability
are satisfied. Reassess the affected scenario rather than calling the entire
system secure from this one change.

A useful follow-up states a concrete question: “Which identities can read the
roster export, under what rule, and what actual check supports that?” Another
asks how staff would retrieve the roster if the normal path were unavailable
during check-in. These are proposed evaluations. This lesson neither executes
an access attempt nor selects an operational protection design.

### 4) **Common confusion**

A security concern is not necessarily about secrecy. Public information can be
harmful when altered, and a correct private file can be unusable at the required
time. Likewise, a possible event and an observed incident are different evidence
claims. Preserve the “could happen” wording until observations support more.

Risk is not just the severity of a named weakness. Two deployments can expose the
same weakness differently and suffer different consequences. “No incidents known”
can also reflect limited observation; it is not by itself proof of no meaningful
risk. Explain the assessment's scope and uncertainty.

### 5) **Performance note**

Availability depends partly on timely usable service, so resource exhaustion or
slow work can matter to a security scenario. A slow response alone does not identify
an attack. Protective checks, encryption and monitoring can also add work whose
cost depends on implementation and workload. No latency, capacity, overhead or
risk-reduction measurement was performed here; the CIA labels themselves provide
no performance guarantee.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask the learner to name the valuable thing, affected person, harmful event and
property at stake before suggesting a tool. Give them the public-catalog variation
and ask which conclusion changes. A sound answer keeps public integrity and
check-in availability visible while withdrawing only the file's stated private-
roster disclosure concern. It does not invent a probability or an incident.
