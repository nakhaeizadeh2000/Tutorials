## 7.1. Personal data privacy consent retention and data minimization

A workshop service encrypts its attendee list, but also collects passport numbers
it never uses and keeps old exports indefinitely. Encryption addresses one
protection question. It does not explain why the organization collects those
fields, what people were told or how long each copy remains necessary.

This lesson teaches vocabulary and constructed policy reasoning. Legal meanings
need an applicable jurisdiction and processing context. UK and EU sources below
are labeled examples; the workshop packet does not determine anyone's legal
obligations or compliance.

### 1) **Identify people-related information and its processing purpose**

**Personal data** broadly concerns information relating to an identified or
identifiable person. The precise legal definition and scope depend on the relevant
framework. For a concrete UK example, the ICO's
[personal-information guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/personal-information-what-is-it/what-is-personal-information-a-guide/)
explains both the relationship to an individual and direct or indirect
identifiability. Knowing a legal name is not always necessary for identification.
A booking identifier linked to an attendee can matter even when the report omits
the attendee's name. Public availability also does not by itself remove the need
to examine the information and its use.

**Processing** includes operations with data, such as collection, use, storage,
sharing and deletion. Identify the **purpose**: the task or reason for which the
data is processed. “We have a database” describes a mechanism, not a purpose.
“Contact booked attendees if their workshop is cancelled” supplies a concrete
purpose against which fields and access can be evaluated.

**Privacy** concerns how data-related operations affect people and the expectations,
rights and responsibilities relevant to those operations. It overlaps with
security while asking additional questions. NIST's
[Privacy Framework 1.0 discussion](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.01162020.pdf)
explains that privacy risks can arise without a cybersecurity incident. A service
can restrict database access correctly while using collected information in a
way that creates privacy problems. Conversely, a sensible collection purpose
does not replace protection against unauthorized disclosure.

**Pseudonymization** separates a person's direct identity from a working reference
while retaining a way to link the information, such as a separately controlled
mapping. **Anonymization** aims to make the information no longer relate to an
identifiable person under the applicable assessment. Removing names alone does
not establish that result: other fields or retained mappings can identify people.
The ICO guide distinguishes pseudonymized and truly anonymous information in
its UK GDPR context.

Encrypting an attendee record does not automatically anonymize it when the system
can recover its contents. A hash of a predictable email address likewise does not
prove anonymity or confidentiality. The earlier cryptography lesson explains
why guessing and linking remain different questions from decrypting a value.
Ask who has additional information and which means of identification are
reasonably relevant to the assessment, rather than demanding an impossible
proof about every hypothetical observer.

### 2) **Separate consent necessary collection and justified retention**

**Consent** is an individual's agreement to specified processing under the relevant
conditions. A recorded checkbox is an interface artifact; its meaning depends
on the choice and information actually provided. Do not turn “there is a checkbox”
into “every later use is permitted.”

For an EU GDPR example, the European Data Protection Board's
[lawful-processing guide](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en)
explains that consent is one possible legal basis, alongside others. When relying
on consent, it must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, with
withdrawal available. A preselected box is not the required affirmative action.
These are scoped legal concepts, not a claim that every data operation everywhere
requires consent or that selecting any other basis is automatically valid.
Determining a real basis needs the actual purpose, circumstances and law.

**Data minimization** limits collection and use to data adequate and relevant for
the stated purpose, without unnecessary detail. Minimum does not mean too little
to perform the task. The ICO's [minimization explanation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/data-minimisation/)
connects sufficiency and necessity in its UK context. In engineering discussions,
ask what each field enables. “Maybe useful later” does not explain a current
need for a passport number in a cancellation-mail workflow.

**Retention** concerns how long data is kept and the rules for reviewing, deleting
or otherwise disposing of it. A **retention period** needs an object, purpose,
starting event and applicable exceptions. Thirty days after an event, thirty days
after collection and thirty days after account closure are different rules.
No single duration in this lesson is a universal legal or operational default.

The ICO's [storage-limitation guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/storage-limitation/)
connects duration to purpose and distinguishes deletion from taking data offline.
Logs, exports, replicas and backups can contain additional copies. Removing a row
from the primary table does not demonstrate disposal of every copy. A documented
exception also needs its own purpose, scope, authority and controls; neither
“backup” nor “analytics” explains indefinite retention by itself.

These ICO pages state that their guidance is under review following the UK's
Data (Use and Access) Act. Use the current applicable text and qualified assessment
for a real decision. Their conceptual examples here do not silently substitute
UK rules for EU or another jurisdiction's rules.

### 3) **Review a collection and deletion claim against a stated policy**

Read this **fictional product and evidence packet**:

| Record | Supplied fact |
|---|---|
| Operational purpose | Email booked attendees about cancellation of their selected workshop |
| Required link | Booking ID connects the attendee's email to the workshop |
| Proposed form | Collects booking ID, email, full date of birth and passport number |
| Current needs assessment | No age, travel or identity-document task; date of birth and passport number are unused |
| Product's chosen marketing rule | Separate optional affirmative agreement is required before adding an attendee to marketing mail |
| Form observation | Marketing box is preselected; no later affirmative choice is recorded |
| Operational retention policy | Delete cancellation-contact records 30 days after the workshop ends; this packet has no exception for these records |
| At day 31 after workshop end | Primary records were removed, but an identifiable CSV export remains in a shared folder |

The booking/workshop link and email support the operational purpose. The unused
birth date and passport number have no demonstrated need under the supplied
assessment. Ask to remove those proposed fields or document a legitimate new
requirement for evaluation. Encrypting them would not establish that necessity.
This is an assessment of the packet's purpose and facts, not a claim that those
fields can never be justified in another service.

The marketing row supplies no affirmative agreement under the product's own
rule. The cancellation contact purpose does not silently supply that separate
choice. The EU consent example explains why a preselected box is also a misleading
shortcut when interpreting consent; the packet does not assess every legal basis
or decide a real marketing-law question.

The CSV contains an additional identifiable copy of the contact records. Under
the stated no-exception policy, the day-31 retention obligation is not satisfied
by deleting only the primary table rows. Identify the export's owner, contents,
access and disposal evidence. A claim that “the database is clean” does not
answer what happened to the shared file.

Now replace the CSV with a workshop-level count whose supplied assessment confirms
that it contains no identifying details and no reasonably usable linkage to
attendees. That changes the evidence about this particular remaining copy. It
does not prove that all logs, backups or other exports were assessed and disposed
of correctly. “We removed the names” alone would not supply the stipulated
anonymity assessment.

Practice: add an adult-only participation requirement. Does that automatically
justify collecting full birth dates and passport numbers for every attendee?
No. The purpose changed, so reassess what evidence and detail are necessary.
A suitable age-eligibility process may need different information; the new
requirement does not itself select the most intrusive fields or settle the
legal basis and retention period. A sound answer asks those additional questions.

### 4) **Common confusion**

Confidential, encrypted, pseudonymized and anonymous describe different things.
A restricted coded record can still be personal data, and an encrypted unnecessary
field can still create an unjustified collection problem. A consent label also
does not prove the conditions for a particular agreement were met.

Retention is not the same as database durability. Durability asks whether stored
information survives a specified failure; retention asks whether and how long it
should remain available. A successful restore can therefore reintroduce records
that require careful treatment under the applicable disposal policy.

### 5) **Performance note**

Collecting and retaining fewer unnecessary fields can reduce stored, transferred
and copied data, but it does not establish an application speedup. Disposal across
exports, logs and backup lifecycles also requires operational work. The effect
depends on volume, architecture, processing and policy.

Privacy terms are not performance metrics. No database deletion, anonymization,
mail choice, export search, restore or benchmark was performed for this packet.
Its 30-day period is an invented policy condition, not a measured retention system.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Have the learner pair every field with a stated purpose and every retained copy
with an owner and disposal rule. Then present the coded CSV without its mapping
and ask what remains unknown about identifiability. A strong answer requests the
missing context rather than calling it anonymous. Finally, change the purpose
and require a fresh necessity analysis instead of copying the original decision.
