## 6.1. OLTP OLAP warehouses lakes ETL ELT and CDC

A reservation screen asks whether one booking is confirmed. A weekly report asks
how many seats were confirmed across all workshops. Both use booking data, but
they need different operations, meanings and freshness. Moving data into an
analytics system introduces another path whose outcome must be checked.

Read the model, query, transaction and copy lessons. The following pipeline and
time cards are constructed interpretation exercises, not a deployed data service.
Documentation was checked 2026-10-18; the named CDC reference is Debezium 3.7 stable.

### 1) **Distinguish operational work from analysis and repository roles**

**OLTP**, online transaction processing, concerns day-to-day operational work such
as recording a booking, changing its status or retrieving it for a user. **OLAP**,
online analytical processing, concerns analysis across data, such as comparing
weekly booking totals by workshop. These are workload orientations, not a rule
that a product can perform only one kind of work. “Online” here does not mean
that the database must be a public website.

Microsoft's [OLTP](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/data-guide/relational-data/online-transaction-processing)
and [OLAP](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/data-guide/relational-data/online-analytical-processing)
guides contrast operational interactions with aggregation and analysis. A small
operational database can also answer a report. Separating workloads becomes a
design question about requirements and resource interference, not an acronym rule.
**HTAP**, hybrid transactional and analytical processing, names approaches that
support both workload kinds in a coordinated system; the label alone does not
establish their isolation or freshness guarantees.

A **data warehouse** organizes data for analysis and reporting, commonly integrating
sources with defined business meanings. A **data mart** serves a narrower subject
or audience, such as workshop sales. A **data lake** retains varied datasets,
often including raw representations, for multiple later uses. It still needs
metadata, access rules and quality management. The
[data-lake guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/data-guide/scenarios/data-lake)
describes raw, cleaned and curated layers. “Lake” does not mean every dataset is
untransformed forever or that throwing files into a bucket produces usable analysis.

**Schema-on-write** emphasizes applying an expected structure when data is stored;
**schema-on-read** emphasizes interpreting structure when it is consumed. Real
pipelines can apply checks at both stages. A **lakehouse** combines lake-style
storage with managed table and analytical capabilities. Microsoft's
[warehouse overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/data-warehouse/data-warehousing)
provides a scoped example of a warehouse on a lake foundation. These categories
can overlap; do not infer one universal physical storage format or performance
level from them.

### 2) **Follow extraction loading transformation and captured changes**

A **data pipeline** connects steps that collect, move or process data. The general
pipeline concept already introduced for tools applies here to data dependencies.
**Ingestion** brings data into a system. **Transformation** changes representation,
selection or derived meaning, such as converting units or computing a total.

**ETL** means **extract, transform, load**. Extract selected source data, transform
it to the required form, then load the result into the designated destination.
**ELT** means **extract, load, transform**: load extracted material into the target
platform first, then transform it there. The
[ETL/ELT comparison](https://aws.amazon.com/compare/the-difference-between-etl-and-elt/)
illustrates that ordering distinction. State the relevant target: a complex
pipeline may use ETL on one boundary and ELT on another. Neither acronym requires
that all work occur in one batch or establishes which approach is faster.

For a constructed report, the source contains these three records for the same
reporting day:

| Booking | Status | Seats |
|---|---|---|
| r17 | approved | 2 |
| r18 | cancelled | 1 |
| r19 | approved | 3 |

The product rule is “sum seats for approved bookings.” Transforming these records
produces **5 approved seats**, not three seats and not six. Five counts seats;
three counts all booking records; six sums seats without the status filter.
Define the metric before naming the pipeline.

An ETL version computes five before loading a summary record. An ELT version loads
the three source records first and computes five inside the destination platform.
Both should satisfy the same stated metric for this input. Their order does not
change its meaning, although their retained raw data and operating costs can differ.

**CDC**, change data capture, identifies source-data changes and makes them available
to another process. It can feed a pipeline; it is not a synonym for transforming
or fully applying every change downstream. Debezium's
[stable documentation](https://debezium.io/documentation/reference/3.7/)
describes database changes as event streams. A **connector** integrates a source
or destination with that movement system.

Its [PostgreSQL connector guide](https://debezium.io/documentation/reference/3.7/connectors/postgresql.html)
distinguishes a configured initial snapshot from subsequent row-level changes.
It also documents possible duplicate events during recovery. Capture support,
retained source history and restart behavior must be checked for the actual
connector and configuration. “CDC enabled” does not prove that a dashboard has
applied the latest change or that processing an event twice is harmless.

### 3) **Diagnose a fresh-source and old-report disagreement**

This constructed timeline uses one day and UTC throughout:

| Time | Established observation |
|---|---|
| 10:00 | Dashboard's applied input contains the three rows above and displays 5 seats |
| 10:05 | Source commits r17 changing from approved to cancelled |
| 10:05:02 | Capture component records the change event |
| 10:08 | Dashboard still displays 5; no destination-application evidence is supplied |

The source's new approved-seat total would be three for these records, assuming
nothing else changed. The old dashboard value does not disprove the source
transaction. Capture at 10:05:02 also does not establish that the destination or
summary applied the change. Identify the last applied source position or time,
the transformation's completion and which summary the dashboard actually read.

If a new row states “destination applied r17's cancellation, but the report sums
all statuses,” the diagnosis changes: investigate the metric's filtering logic.
If the destination has not applied it, investigate the movement path. The same
visible number can arise from different failures, so inspect the relevant stage.

A **batch** processes a bounded group of data, while **stream processing** handles
an ongoing flow. These names do not supply a universal latency threshold. A stream
can fall behind; a scheduled batch can finish promptly for its requirements.
A **watermark** or progress marker needs its system-specific meaning: a source
position, ingestion time and completed report time are different evidence.

Practice: an implementation increments a total once for every CDC event. It sees
creation and later cancellation of `r17`. Does “two events” mean two confirmed
bookings? No. The events describe changes; the metric describes qualifying current
state. It needs correct interpretation of changes, identity and repeated events,
not blind counting of incoming messages.

### 4) **Common confusion**

A warehouse is not merely any large database, and a lake is not merely any large
file. Their roles involve how datasets are retained, interpreted and served.
Likewise, a nightly report is not automatically a backup that can restore the
operational application. It may omit detail or deliberately retain a different
historical meaning.

CDC is also not a business-event specification by itself. A row update may be part
of a larger operation, and the captured fields do not explain every business intent.
Keep capture, transformation, application and presentation as separate boundaries.

### 5) **Performance note**

Broad analytical scans can compete with operational work for resources. A separate
analytical path may move that work but introduces copying, transformation, storage
and freshness costs. ELT can use destination compute, while ETL can reduce what
is loaded; neither label establishes a cheaper or faster result. This lesson
checks a small sum and a fictional timeline, with no throughput, CDC delay or
analytics benchmark. Compare equivalent metrics and completion boundaries.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask a learner to write the approved-seat rule without acronyms and calculate its
answer before drawing a pipeline. Then mark where the 10:05 change was committed,
captured, applied and displayed. A sound explanation preserves the missing evidence
and can distinguish a transport backlog from a transformation error. Ask how the
answer changes if an event is delivered twice.
