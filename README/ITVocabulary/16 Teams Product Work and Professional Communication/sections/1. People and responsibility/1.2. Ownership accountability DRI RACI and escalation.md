## 1.2. Ownership accountability DRI RACI and escalation

Two people say “I thought the other team owned it.” A name in a tracker can help,
but only when it identifies the outcome, authority and follow-up expected of that
person. This lesson builds on stakeholder and role distinctions. Its assignments
and escalation are fictional; nothing is sent to a real colleague.

### 1) **State the responsibility and its authority boundary**

**Ownership** means assigned stewardship of some defined work or outcome. Specify
what is owned: code maintenance, a service's operation, a product decision or a
particular correction. Owning a repository does not automatically include accepting
customer requirements or approving every release.

**Responsibility** identifies duties or work to perform. **Accountability** identifies
answerability for the relevant result or decision. Those words overlap in ordinary
English; structured methods give them specific meanings. In the RACI model below,
R and A are deliberately distinct. State the model before interpreting the letters.

An accountable person may coordinate several contributors rather than implement
every step. Conversely, assigning someone accountability without access to the
needed people, resources or decision route leaves an important gap. Record that
gap and resolve it; the assignment does not create technical permissions or make
an unavailable person available.

For example, “Sam owns recovery” is too broad to operate. “Sam coordinates the
export-service recovery rehearsal, obtains the database team's restore evidence,
and reports whether the agreed workflow criteria were met” gives a usable scope.
It still needs agreed decision rights, timing and a fallback for absence. Shared
team accountability can coexist with named action owners; avoid making every
participant independently responsible for every detail.

### 2) **Read a responsibility matrix with its legend**

A **DRI**, or **Directly Responsible Individual**, is a named person accountable
for driving a specified activity or outcome under the organization's convention.
The [GitLab handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/people-group/directly-responsible-individuals/)
provides an actual implementation with consultation and task coordination. It also
describes cases where final approval belongs elsewhere. Therefore, DRI does not
universally mean sole implementer, line manager or unrestricted final approver.

**RACI** expands to **Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed**. The
[NSW Public Service Commission template](https://www.psc.nsw.gov.au/assets/psc/documents/raci_model.pdf)
supplies a compact example legend. Here we use the following local interpretation:

| Letter | This lesson's meaning | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| R | Performs the assigned activity | Authority to accept every result |
| A | Answers for this activity's outcome and specified decision | Personally performs every step |
| C | Provides input before the decision or action | Automatic veto or an approval signature |
| I | Receives the relevant decision or outcome | Consultation has already occurred |

A common arrangement names one A for each row so the decision is clear. The R
and A can be the same person, and several people can do the work. Use the local
legend and resolve ambiguous shared authority explicitly. A RACI chart records
assignments; it does not grant API access, enforce a deadline or prove anyone has
completed the activity. Do not silently infer that a DRI must map to R merely
because the word “responsible” appears in its expansion.

### 3) **Escalate an unresolved decision with a usable handoff**

**Escalation** routes an issue to a person or group with the needed authority,
expertise or response capability. It may move to a specialist rather than upward
in a reporting hierarchy. An urgent service incident also needs its incident
process; a general responsibility chart is not a replacement for that process.

Read this fictional assignment packet. Its rule says Ravi decides normal export
scope; changes to retained personal-data purposes require Dana's approval. Ravi
coordinates the proposal and Jo implements accepted changes. Ada supplies task
input; support receives the final decision.

| Activity | R | A | C | I |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decide ordinary export scope | Ravi | Ravi | Ada and Jo | Support |
| Approve a new retention purpose | Ravi prepares the request | Dana | Ada and privacy specialist | Jo and support |
| Implement accepted export change | Jo | Jo | Ravi and Ada | Support |

At 10:00, Jo finds that the requested export would retain additional personal
information for a new purpose. Ravi writes “I'm the DRI; proceed.” Under these
supplied rules, Ravi can drive the request but cannot replace Dana's approval.
Consultation with Ada also cannot replace it. The implementing assignment is
conditional on an accepted change, not permission to resolve the missing decision
by changing software first.

A useful escalation says: “The requested fields introduce a new retention purpose.
Dana owns that approval under our rule. Ravi will prepare the purpose, required
fields, alternatives and impact; Jo needs the decision before implementing those
fields. If Dana is unavailable, use the named policy delegate.” State the actual
time constraint and fallback from policy rather than inventing one from a title.

Practice: Dana acknowledges receipt but supplies no decision. Is the dependency
resolved? No. Receipt establishes communication, not approval. Record the unresolved
decision and next follow-up. If a verified delegation assigns Ravi this exact
approval for the relevant period, his decision may become authorized within that
scope. That changes authority evidence; it does not prove implementation or
privacy compliance. No real policy or legal assessment is made in this exercise.

### 4) **Common confusion**

Responsibility assignment is not blame assignment. Investigating a failure still
requires evidence about conditions, actions and mechanisms. A named owner does
not prove the owner caused a fault or should execute every recovery action alone.

Consulted and informed are different participation obligations. Copying someone
after a decision does not satisfy a required prior consultation. Equally, seeking
input does not automatically give every respondent a veto. Read the decision rules.

### 5) **Performance note**

A RACI label has no direct runtime cost or speed benefit. Clear routes can affect
waiting for decisions, while unnecessary approvals can add delay. Measure that
workflow if it matters; assigning more letters to a chart guarantees no reduction.
No escalation-response,delivery-effort or application-performance measurement was
performed here. Service recovery timing remains a separate operational question.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask “Who does the work, who can make this decision, and what evidence closes it?”
Require a separate answer for the ordinary-scope and retention-purpose rows. Then
add only the acknowledgment. A sound explanation keeps the dependency open and
uses the agreed fallback without confusing seniority,receipt and authorization.
