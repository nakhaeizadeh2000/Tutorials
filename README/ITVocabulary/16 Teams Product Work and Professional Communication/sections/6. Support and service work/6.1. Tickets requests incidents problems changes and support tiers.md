## 6.1. Tickets requests incidents problems changes and support tiers

“Ticket closed; problem solved” can hide several different outcomes. A person may
have regained access while the cause of a recurring outage remains unknown. Read
the record, its work type and its completion evidence separately.

### 1) **Separate the record from the work**

A **ticket** is a tracked record of work or a request. It can hold observations,
conversation, assignment, links and status. Creating one does not establish that
a software defect exists. A question and an outage can both have tickets while
requiring different responses.

A **service request** asks for information, access or something to be provided,
such as an approved account. An **incident** concerns an unplanned disruption or
reduction in service quality; the existing [incident lesson](<../../../15 Performance Observability and Reliability/sections/6. Incident operations/6.1. Alerts incidents severity priority mitigation and remediation.md>)
owns impact, severity and restoration vocabulary. **Problem management** investigates
causes or potential causes of incidents and how recurrence might be prevented.
The everyday sentence “I have a problem” does not identify that formal work type.

A **change** is an alteration to a service or its supporting environment, managed
under the applicable change process. Changing a production configuration and
fulfilling an ordinary information request are different work, even if linked.
A request may require a change, and a change may cause an incident. These are
relationships between activities, not mutually exclusive descriptions of an entire
conversation. [Atlassian's work categories](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-service-management-cloud/docs/what-are-ticket-categories/)
provide one concrete service-tool vocabulary; their product categories and workflow
settings are not a universal classification law.

### 2) **Read triage and support routing**

**Triage** initially assesses what is happening, impact, urgency and who should
handle it. It is not necessarily diagnosis. Ask which service and users are
involved, when the behavior occurred, what evidence exists and whether it is
ongoing. Use the agreed severity and priority definitions rather than inferring
urgency from a ticket's capitalization.

**Reproduction** attempts to observe the reported behavior under stated conditions.
The [reproducible issue lesson](<../../../12 Testing Debugging and Quality Evidence/sections/5. Applied reporting and review/5.1. Write a reproducible issue with expected and actual behavior.md>) owns how to record
expected and actual results. “Could not reproduce in staging” narrows the evidence
to those conditions; it does not show the production report is false. Investigation
and restoration may proceed before a complete reproduction exists.

A **support tier** or **level** groups responsibilities or expertise in a local
support arrangement. A common arrangement uses first-contact staff, specialists
and engineering or vendor support. Level zero sometimes denotes self-service.
Read the actual policy: numbers, permissions, hours and routing differ. A vendor's
[support-level overview](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/incident-response/support-levels)
is an example of an arrangement, not evidence that every company uses its duties
or connects a particular tier to a particular incident severity.

Moving to specialist expertise is a form of functional escalation. Involving a
manager for authority or coordination is a different escalation need. A major
outage may require immediate on-call involvement under local policy; do not
invent a requirement to wait through every tier. Keep a named follow-up owner
while routing and record what the recipient accepted. Sending a ticket is not
proof that anyone has taken responsibility.

### 3) **Classify a linked support packet**

Consider these fictional records. No service was operated for this example.

| Record | Supplied evidence |
|---|---|
| T21 | New employee requests an account; no existing service failure reported |
| I8 | Existing staff cannot sign in from 10:00; at 10:20 the response team records restored sign-in after a configuration reversal |
| P3 | Linked investigation asks why the configuration was accepted; cause not established |
| C9 | Proposed replacement configuration awaits required review and approval |
| Routing note | Local policy sends ongoing sign-in outages directly to identity on-call; first-contact staff retain requester communication |

T21 is a service request on this evidence. I8 is an incident with a recorded
restoration outcome. That does not close P3: restoration does not establish the
cause or eliminate recurrence. C9 is proposed change work, not an applied fix.
The same configuration can appear in all three records without their statuses
becoming interchangeable.

A supported update is: “Sign-in was recorded restored at 10:20 after reversal.
P3 remains open to investigate why the configuration was accepted. C9 is awaiting
review and approval. First-contact staff own requester updates; identity on-call
owns the routed outage response under the supplied policy.” It does not claim
a permanent fix, an approved replacement or universal impact.

Now change only I8's last observation to “one staff member signed in successfully.”
The evidence no longer supports service-wide restoration. Keep the positive
observation but ask for the recovery checks and population that justify closure.
Next, remove the routing policy. The packet then supports asking who should take
response ownership; it does not establish that identity on-call accepted it.

### 4) **Common confusion**

A support tier is not an application architecture tier, incident severity or a
customer's purchased service tier. “Tier two” needs its local meaning. Likewise,
“resolved” may describe a ticket workflow transition rather than a demonstrated
permanent correction. Read linked work and closure criteria before summarizing it.

### 5) **Performance note**

Classification does not directly accelerate a service. Better routing can affect
response work, but response time and application latency measure different things.
The packet supplies restoration timestamps, not workload, latency or a measured
comparison proving that a support-tier arrangement is faster.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask the learner to connect each record to its intended outcome and missing evidence.
Then replace the restoration statement with the single successful sign-in. A good
answer revises the recovery claim while leaving the pending investigation and
change statuses intact. Do not reward guesses about a root cause.
