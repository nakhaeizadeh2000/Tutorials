## 7.1. B2B B2C tenants KPIs OKRs ROI TCO CapEx and OpEx

“Our B2B SaaS has fifty tenants and positive ROI” combines a customer relationship,
a service grouping and a financial claim. None tells you how many people use it,
whether customer data is isolated, or which costs were counted. Read each claim
at its own boundary.

### 1) **Identify the customer and tenant boundary**

**Business-to-business (B2B)** describes serving another business as a customer;
**business-to-consumer (B2C)** describes serving individual consumers. The buyer,
administrator and daily user may be different people. A workshop platform sold
to training companies can be B2B even when individuals attend the workshops.
These labels do not specify deployment architecture or contract size.

A **tenant** is a customer or group boundary defined by the service. A company
might have one tenant with many users, or separate tenants for different business
units. Read which identity, configuration, billing and data boundaries the term
actually denotes. **Multitenancy** involves serving multiple tenants; it does not
by itself establish a particular database layout or adequate isolation.
[Microsoft's multitenant overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/guide/multitenant/overview)
explicitly distinguishes tenants from users and application tenants from directory
tenants. Preserve that context when reading a vendor diagram.

Fifty tenants therefore does not mean fifty users or fifty servers. Nor does
“SaaS” prove the provider owns every access decision. The existing service-model
lesson explains [shared responsibility](<../../../14 Infrastructure Cloud and Delivery/sections/1. Hosting and responsibility/1.2. IaaS PaaS SaaS managed services and shared responsibility.md>); this lesson identifies
whose business and service boundaries a statement concerns.

### 2) **Separate indicators goals and cost language**

A **key performance indicator (KPI)** is a selected measure used to assess an
important objective or aspect of performance. “Successful exports per eligible
export attempt” and “monthly subscription revenue” measure different things.
Define numerator, denominator, population, time window and collection method.
An attractive number can rise while user outcomes worsen if the measure excludes
the people who failed before reaching its recorded step.

**Objectives and key results (OKRs)** connect a desired direction or outcome with
specific results used to assess progress. The [What Matters introduction](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/okr-meaning-definition-example)
explains this objective/result relationship; its recommended formats are not universal policy. “Make workshop administration dependable”
is an objective; “Raise the weekly successful-export fraction from 90% to 98%
for the defined eligible attempts by quarter end” is a candidate key result.
The measure may also be monitored as a KPI. Installing a dashboard is an activity;
it does not establish that the export outcome improved. Goal-setting vocabulary
does not demonstrate that a proposed intervention caused a result.

**Return on investment (ROI)** compares a net benefit with an investment cost,
commonly as a percentage. State the formula because benefit, cost and time horizon
can differ across reports. **Total cost of ownership (TCO)** asks what acquiring,
operating, supporting and eventually changing or retiring an option costs over
a defined scope and period. A monthly hosting bill alone omits other possible
costs. The [FinOps terminology guide](https://www.finops.org/assets/terminology/)
provides useful technology-cost context; estimates still need their own inputs.

**Capital expenditure (CapEx)** and **operating expenditure (OpEx)** concern how
spending is classified and recognized. Acquiring equipment for use over time and
paying an ongoing service bill illustrate different patterns. Neither “upfront”
nor “cloud” decides classification on its own. Applicable accounting rules and
the organization's finance policy determine treatment. Here the task is to read
and question a cost claim, not classify an actual purchase or recommend one.

### 3) **Challenge a business case with evidence**

Consider this original fictional proposal, using invented cost units:

| Item | Supplied assumption or observation |
|---|---|
| Customers | Two training companies; three application tenants; 80 user accounts |
| Outcome | Last week 90 of 100 eligible export attempts succeeded |
| Goal | Reach 98% by the end of the quarter for the same defined population |
| One-year option cost | Build 20; operation 12; support 8; no other costs included |
| One-year benefit | Estimated 50, conditional on adoption and saved work |
| Summary | “Three users; 98% achieved; ROI 150%; cloud makes all costs OpEx” |

The summary confuses tenants with accounts and a target with an observed result.
The recorded fraction is 90/100 = 90%. Under the explicitly limited cost model,
cost is 20 + 12 + 8 = 40. If ROI means `(benefit − cost) / cost`, its estimate is
(50 − 40) / 40 = 25%, not 150%. This is conditional arithmetic, not a validated
business return. Ask whether migration, training and other material costs are
missing and whether benefit is realizable in the stated year.

Now reduce the estimated benefit to 30 while retaining cost 40. The same formula
gives −25%. Changing the assumption changes the estimate without changing the
observed export fraction or finance classification. A responsible rewrite keeps
those claims separate: “Three tenants contain 80 accounts. The current measured
fraction is 90%; 98% is the target. The limited one-year ROI estimate is 25% under
the stated benefit assumption; missing costs and finance treatment need review.”

### 4) **Common confusion**

A KPI is not inherently a target, and a key result is not automatically proof of
causation. Revenue is not profit, and avoided staff time is not automatically a
cash saving. Ask what the benefit represents before using it in an ROI formula.
A lower vendor price also does not establish lower TCO.

### 5) **Performance note**

Business indicators can include latency or reliability, but naming a KPI does not
improve either. Compare the same workload and service outcome when relating cost
to performance. This packet contains an outcome fraction and hypothetical costs;
it contains no timings, service optimization or demonstrated financial return.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask the learner to underline observations, targets and estimates in different
ways, then explain the changed-benefit calculation. A strong answer preserves
all three user/tenant/customer counts and declines to infer accounting treatment
from the hosting label. Reward explicit assumptions rather than a confident purchase
recommendation.
