## 4.2. Dependencies blockers scope creep Definition of Done and handoffs

“Development done; blocked on QA; handed over.” This can hide an incomplete product,
a vague dependency and a responsibility gap. A useful update names the completed
scope, the work that remains and the person who will act next. Use the previous
planning lesson to keep waiting separate from effort.

### 1) **Describe what prevents which outcome**

A **work dependency** is a prerequisite relationship: outcome B requires some input,
decision or result A. A dependency can be satisfied, pending or uncertain. It is
not automatically a problem. An export may depend on an agreed list of columns;
if that decision exists, the dependency is satisfied.

A **blocker** currently prevents a specified activity or required outcome from
proceeding. A pending provider approval can block production access while still
allowing a local UI investigation. “The project is blocked” overstates the condition
if useful independent work remains. State the affected path and what would unblock it.

A **risk** concerns a possible harmful outcome, rather than an established current
obstacle. An approval that might arrive late is a delivery risk. If the needed
approval is absent when the dependent activity must start, it may also be a current
blocker. These categories can describe different aspects of one situation; avoid
mechanically converting every risk into a red status or every dependency into blame.

Use a concrete statement: “The production-provider integration cannot start until
access request A7 is accepted. Priya owns follow-up; the local selection check can
continue with synthetic data.” This identifies the outcome, missing condition,
owner and independent work. “Waiting on the provider” supplies much less actionable
information. Follow the agreed escalation route when the current owner cannot
resolve the decision; do not infer authority from seniority alone.

### 2) **Keep scope and completion criteria visible**

**Scope** identifies the included outcomes and boundaries of the work. A **scope
change** revises them. **Scope creep** commonly describes expansion that is not
adequately recognized, evaluated or agreed. Some organizations use the term for
any expansion; clarify their usage before declaring every new requirement improper.

For example, adding scheduled email delivery to an agreed download-only export
introduces a new outcome. It may be valuable. Make the added identity, data,
operational and delivery duties visible, then agree scope and timing with the
relevant owner. Quietly accepting the request while retaining the old forecast
hides a decision. Refusing every change merely because it is new can also prevent
useful adaptation. Atlassian's original [team accounts of managing scope](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/development/4-how-tos-dealing-with-scope-creep)
illustrate making changed work and trade-offs explicit; their historical tools
and team rituals are not required for this reasoning.

A **Definition of Done**, or **DoD**, defines a shared completion standard. In
[Scrum 2020](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html#commitment-definition-of-done)
it describes the required quality state of the Increment. A local non-Scrum team
can also use the phrase, but should state the scope. “Code written” is not the
same as meeting a standard that also requires review, integration checks and
usable instructions. A tracker label does not override missing evidence.

Item-specific acceptance criteria describe the particular outcome, such as exporting
only pending W1 records. A shared DoD can require relevant checks for every addition.
Both can apply: a correct W1 sample does not satisfy an unperformed required
integration check, and a general review checklist does not replace W1's selection
rule. Scrum.org's [DoD explanation](https://www.scrum.org/resources/definition-done)
reinforces usable quality rather than completion by a status change. Done is
neither a proof of no defects nor automatically a record of production deployment.

### 3) **Transfer unfinished work without inventing completion**

A **handoff** transfers responsibility together with enough context for the recipient
to act. It needs the actual object and version, current state, relevant evidence,
known unknowns, next actions and receiving responsibility. GitLab's
[on-call handoff guide](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/development/sec/oncall/handoff-and-continuity/)
is an operational example of preserving impact, attempted work, blockers and next
steps. General product work uses the same continuity questions without inheriting
that team's shift schedule or messaging procedure.

Read this fictional packet:

| Record | Supplied information |
|---|---|
| Agreed S8 scope | Download selected-workshop pending contacts; no scheduled email |
| Local DoD | Reviewed implementation, required real-provider integration check and support instructions |
| Candidate H8 | Code and review complete; synthetic selection check passes |
| Remaining evidence | Real-provider access unavailable; support instructions not written |
| New request | Sponsor asks to add scheduled email without changing the date |
| Handoff | Jo posts “done, QA to finish”; no recipient has accepted the next assignment |

H8's implementation and review are complete under the packet. S8 does not meet
its local DoD. The real-provider check is blocked by access; instruction writing
is unfinished but has no supplied access blocker. Scheduled email is proposed
additional scope, not part of the accepted S8 outcome. “QA to finish” neither
assigns a named recipient nor proves responsibility transferred.

A useful handoff draft is:

> H8 code and review are complete; its synthetic selection check passes. The
> required real-provider check is blocked on access. Priya owns access follow-up;
> Lee is the proposed integration-check recipient and must confirm that assignment
> under our handoff process. Jo still owns support instructions. S8 remains
> incomplete under our DoD. Ravi will resolve the email-scheduling scope request
> and its effect on timing before it is added.

This draft names proposed versus established responsibilities. It is not a message
sent to anyone. If the recipient cannot accept the work, use the local fallback
rather than treating silence as acceptance. A file upload or a copied channel is
not itself evidence that another person can and will act.

Practice: Lee accepts the assignment and access becomes available. The handoff
and access dependency improve, but the provider check is still unperformed and
instructions still unfinished. Later passing that check changes its result only.
Do not mark all remaining conditions complete from one dependency being resolved.

### 4) **Common confusion**

“Not done” does not mean “no progress.” Report completed work precisely while
retaining the larger outcome's unfinished status. Conversely, effort spent or code
merged is not completion when the agreed outcome includes more.

A handoff is also different from a release or delegation of approval authority.
Transferring investigation responsibility does not automatically let the recipient
waive a quality standard or accept a new data-processing purpose.

### 5) **Performance note**

These labels have no direct application-runtime effect. Hidden waiting and repeated
handoffs can consume elapsed delivery time, but a handoff count alone does not
explain the cause or quantify a remedy. Skipping required load checks to meet a
date changes evidence and risk, not demonstrated performance. No handoff, approval,
provider check, scheduling feature or timing experiment was executed for this packet.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask the learner to write three statuses: implementation, whole S8 outcome and
receiving responsibility. A strong answer keeps them different. Then provide
Lee's acknowledgment and available access. They should update the assignment and
dependency without inventing a passing check or completed instructions.
