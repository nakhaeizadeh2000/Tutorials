## 2.1. Requirements constraints acceptance criteria and quality attributes

“The export must be fast and user-friendly. Use a cache.” This mixes desired
qualities with a possible implementation and leaves acceptance undecidable.
Requirements work makes the intended outcome and the permitted solution space
clear enough for people to build and evaluate the right thing.

This lesson applies the earlier requirement-language and acceptance distinctions.
The packet is fictional and requires no running exporter or performance tool.

### 1) **Separate the needed outcome from constraints on the solution**

A **requirement** states a necessary capability, behavior or condition within an
agreed scope. A **functional requirement** describes what the system must do,
such as letting authorized coordinators export pending booking IDs. **Quality
attributes** describe characteristics such as performance, reliability, usability,
security and modifiability. A particular requirement makes one of those concerns
specific enough to evaluate.

**Non-functional requirement** is a common umbrella for quality and other system
requirements. It does not mean optional or less important. Classification varies:
rejecting unauthorized access describes behavior and contributes to security.
Do not spend more effort forcing it into one category than understanding the
required outcome and its conditions.

A **constraint** restricts permitted solutions or operating conditions. “Use the
existing customer identity provider” constrains an integration choice. “Deployment
must use the approved region” restricts placement. Constraints can themselves be
requirements. The useful distinction is what they restrict and why, rather than
a rule that constraints and requirements can never overlap.

By contrast, “a cache might help” is a design suggestion until an authorized
requirement makes that mechanism compulsory. Preserve legitimate constraints,
but ask why a proposed mechanism is needed. Prematurely requiring one can hide
better solutions or conflict with freshness needs. NASA's
[requirements checklist](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/appendix-c-how-to-write-a-good-requirement/)
supports explicit actors, consistent terms, assumptions and verifiability. Its agency
wording conventions do not replace this project's agreed terminology.

### 2) **Make acceptance evidence observable and scoped**

**Acceptance criteria** are conditions for judging an acceptable outcome; the
quality-evidence domain owns their general distinction from acceptance decisions.
Here, connect the criteria to the task. “A button exists” can be inspected, but
that observation alone may not show that a coordinator can obtain the needed list.

For each important quality, state the triggering situation, relevant conditions
and required response. SEI's original [quality-attribute scenario discussion](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/blog/using-quality-attributes-as-a-means-to-improve-acquisition-strategies/)
uses such scenarios to make concerns concrete. This lesson adds explicit outcome
criteria rather than claiming every attribute reduces to one number.

For instance, a proposed export criterion might say: “On the agreed staging setup,
with 1,000 pending records and five simultaneous coordinators, every one of the
20 specified export attempts produces the complete required CSV within three
seconds from clicking Export to completed download.” The numbers are invented
teaching conditions, not recommended targets or test results. An actual plan must
identify the environment, dataset, measurement method and expected content.

“Within three seconds” by itself does not specify where timing begins or ends.
A mean of three seconds is also different from every checked attempt meeting a
three-second bound. Some qualities need structured inspection or user-task evidence
alongside measurements. Choose evidence appropriate to the claim and preserve its
limits; one sample is not proof for all future workloads.

**Requirements traceability** connects a need to requirements and their relevant
design, implementation and evaluation records. An identifier helps keep these
connections stable when wording changes. A link to a test plan means a check was
planned. A linked execution record may support a result for its exact version and
conditions. Neither guarantees that the requirement was complete or appropriate.

### 3) **Repair a vague export requirement without inventing approval**

Read this constructed packet:

| Record | Supplied information |
|---|---|
| Need N4 | Coordinators need current pending-booking contacts for the workshop selected on screen |
| Draft R4 | Export all bookings quickly using a ten-minute cache |
| Constraint C2 | Exports may include only the selected workshop; apply the existing access rules |
| Example | The screen selects W1; the sample file includes pending W1 and W2 records |
| Timing | A plan proposes measuring server generation time; no run is recorded |
| Authority | Ada decides acceptance scope; the technical team proposes implementation options |

R4's “all bookings” is ambiguous and conflicts with C2 if read as all workshops.
The supplied sample violates C2 because it includes W2. That is a known packet
mismatch, independent of the unmeasured speed claim. The generation-time plan
would not by itself establish completed-download time.

A proposed revision is: “For an authorized coordinator selecting W1, export the
pending W1 booking IDs and contact addresses under the agreed data-currentness
rule. Exclude every other workshop.” Ada still needs to agree what “current” means
and which input and timing conditions serve the task. A ten-minute cache might
be acceptable under one rule and unsuitable under another. Do not silently choose
a tolerance and call it customer approval.

A useful review says: “The sample includes W2 despite C2. Please reconcile R4's
selection wording with N4 and C2, define acceptable data age and completed-download
timing, and retain the check results for the candidate version.” This proposes
concrete next work without inventing a measured result or universally banning caches.

Practice: remove the sample row. The requirement ambiguity and missing timing
remain, but the demonstrated W2 output violation disappears from the available
evidence. If a revised sample then excludes W2, that supports the checked selection
only; it does not establish access enforcement, freshness or performance.

### 4) **Common confusion**

An acceptance criterion is not necessarily a single automated test, and a passing
test is not the acceptance decision. A constraint can be legitimate even when it
restricts design freedom. Conversely, a developer's preferred library does not
become a required constraint merely by appearing in a draft.

Detailed wording is not automatically good wording. A perfectly measurable
“export every booking” rule can still serve the wrong task or disclose records
outside the permitted scope. Evaluate the need and boundaries as well as clarity.

### 5) **Performance note**

A performance requirement sets an expectation; it does not change runtime behavior.
Targets influence design choices and the cost of evaluation. Excessively tight
bounds can impose unnecessary engineering work, while loose bounds can miss the
user's time-sensitive task. The three-second example is unmeasured fiction; no
cache, concurrency or export-speed experiment was executed. Existing measurement
lessons own timing boundaries and workload comparisons.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask the learner to underline the needed outcome, circle the imposed boundary and
mark each unverified quality. They should identify the W2 mismatch without claiming
that the exporter is slow. Remove that sample and check whether they correctly
withdraw the observed-violation claim while retaining the requirements questions.
