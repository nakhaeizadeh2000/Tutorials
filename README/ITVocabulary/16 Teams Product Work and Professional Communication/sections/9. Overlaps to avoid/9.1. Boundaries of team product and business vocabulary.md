## 9.1. Boundaries of team product and business vocabulary

Knowing what an ADR or key result means helps you participate in a decision.
It does not authorize the decision or establish that the organization follows
the process you learned. This domain teaches how to read work, ask precise
questions and explain consequences. Applying a company policy requires that
policy and its actual authority.

### 1) **Recognize what the vocabulary establishes**

Use [roles](<../1. People and responsibility/1.1. Stakeholders users customers and technical roles.md>), [responsibility](<../1. People and responsibility/1.2. Ownership accountability DRI RACI and escalation.md>)
and [requirements](<../2. Requirements and product scope/2.1. Requirements constraints acceptance criteria and quality attributes.md>) to identify
whose outcome and decision a statement concerns. Do not turn a job title into an
assumed permission. A responsibility table is evidence of an agreed arrangement
only when its status and scope support that reading.

The method lesson distinguishes [Scrum and flow](<../3. Work methods/3.1. Agile Scrum Kanban Lean iterations and sprints.md>).
It does not prescribe which method your team should adopt or prove that one will
improve delivery. Framework rules, a team's actual agreement and observed practice
can differ. If someone says “Scrum requires individual velocity rankings,” locate
the originating rule rather than accepting an asserted mandate. This domain's
research used the actual Scrum Guide and current Kanban Guide, with those sources
linked at their detailed owner.

Similarly, a cost estimate is a model under assumptions. The business lesson owns
[ROI and TCO](<../7. Business and service context/7.1. B2B B2C tenants KPIs OKRs ROI TCO CapEx and OpEx.md>); accounting classification and an investment decision
require applicable policy, evidence and responsible specialists. Vocabulary
competence supports asking what was counted. It does not establish the answer.

### 2) **Route a practical question to its owner**

| Question | First useful destination | Needed beyond a definition |
|---|---|---|
| Does “done” mean released? | [Progress and handoffs](<../4. Planning and progress/4.2. Dependencies blockers scope creep Definition of Done and handoffs.md>) | Local completion and release rules, observed evidence |
| Who can accept this scope addition? | [Ownership](<../1. People and responsibility/1.2. Ownership accountability DRI RACI and escalation.md>) | Current authority and an explicit decision |
| Is this internal RFC binding? | [Document purposes](<../5. Documents and decisions/5.1. PRDs RFCs ADRs proposals specifications and decision records.md>) | Document status, local adoption and supersession rules |
| Is the service restored? | [Incident terminology](<../../../15 Performance Observability and Reliability/sections/6. Incident operations/6.1. Alerts incidents severity priority mitigation and remediation.md>) | Recovery evidence for the affected service and population |
| Will the proposal pay for itself? | [Business claims](<../7. Business and service context/7.1. B2B B2C tenants KPIs OKRs ROI TCO CapEx and OpEx.md>) | Complete assumptions, costs, benefits and validation |
| How do we implement tenant isolation? | [Shared responsibility](<../../../14 Infrastructure Cloud and Delivery/sections/1. Hosting and responsibility/1.2. IaaS PaaS SaaS managed services and shared responsibility.md>) and dedicated architecture/security study | Threat model, design, implementation and verified access boundaries |

A route is not a substitute for answering the question. Read the owner for the
full distinction, then collect the missing local evidence. A process name should
not force the learner into an unrelated framework course just to understand a
status message. Conversely, a glossary paragraph cannot implement a support
workflow, security control or finance model.

### 3) **Assess a bounded work conversation**

Read this fictional conversation: “The prototype is done. The PM owns it, so the
ADR proves we can release. Our OKR says export success will be 99%, therefore the
new service is reliable and its TCO is low.” No other artifacts are supplied.

A defensible response separates the questions. What was the prototype intended
to establish, and what observed result completes that purpose? Which PM role and
release authority apply here? What decision does the ADR record, in what status?
Is 99% a target or a measured indicator, with which denominator and time window?
What costs, period and alternatives make “low” meaningful? None of those gaps is
filled merely by recognizing the acronyms.

Suppose the next artifact is an accepted ADR choosing a storage option. That
resolves a design decision within the record's scope. It still does not prove
the option is deployed, release criteria passed, reliability measured or costs
validated. A learner who adds those conclusions has crossed an evidence boundary.
The useful next action is to locate the missing artifact or ask its owner.

### 4) **Common confusion**

Local variation does not mean words have no useful meaning. A shared vocabulary
lets you identify which definition is in use and which difference matters. State
the source and context rather than either imposing one company's process or
refusing to interpret anything until every policy is known.

### 5) **Performance note**

This boundary lesson changes no executable behavior. A process improvement may
change coordination, but neither a new ceremony nor a low cost estimate establishes
faster requests. Use the measurement owner for operation, workload, timing and
comparison evidence before making a performance claim.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Have the learner choose one unsupported conclusion in the conversation, state the
smallest missing evidence and identify who could provide it. Then give them only
the accepted ADR. A strong answer narrows one uncertainty without treating the
new artifact as proof of every outcome.
