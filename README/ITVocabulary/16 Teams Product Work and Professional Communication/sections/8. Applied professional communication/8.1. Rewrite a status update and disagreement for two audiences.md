## 8.1. Rewrite a status update and disagreement for two audiences

“Backend done, QA blocking us, should ship Friday” gives the reader a conclusion
without its evidence. A teammate needs enough detail to investigate; a product
stakeholder needs the consequence and decision. Neither audience benefits from
turning an uncertain forecast into a promise.

### 1) **Extract a defensible status**

Use this constructed packet throughout the exercise. These records are invented;
no checks, messages or releases were executed for this lesson.

| Record | Available information |
|---|---|
| Agreed scope | Selected-workshop CSV export; no scheduled email delivery |
| Candidate | Build E7 exists in staging; its recorded synthetic filter check passed |
| Remaining evidence | Shared-provider integration check cannot start until credentials arrive; accessibility review has not started |
| Responsibility | Jo owns the candidate and integration follow-up; Mei owns the accessibility review; Ada decides product scope and release acceptance |
| Dependency | Provider contact has not confirmed a credential delivery date |
| Forecast | Friday assumes credentials by Wednesday and successful remaining checks; neither condition is established |
| Disagreement | A teammate proposes shipping E7 now and adding scheduled email “because customers will expect it” |

Before rewriting, mark what each statement actually supports. A synthetic check
supports its recorded case, not every integration or accessibility outcome.
Missing credentials block one check; they do not explain why the accessibility
review has not started. Jo and Mei own different follow-ups. Ada's acceptance
authority does not establish that she accepted E7 or the email addition.

Use the earlier [planning lesson](<../4. Planning and progress/4.2. Dependencies blockers scope creep Definition of Done and handoffs.md>) for the
full distinction between blockers, remaining work and agreed scope. Here the
new task is preserving those distinctions in a message someone can act on.

### 2) **Adapt detail to two audiences**

A teammate update can retain build identity and the check boundary:

> E7 is in staging. Its recorded synthetic selected-workshop filter check passed.
> The shared-provider integration check is pending credentials, with no confirmed
> arrival date. Mei's accessibility review has not started. Jo will follow up on
> credentials and run the integration check when access is available; Mei owns the
> review. Friday remains conditional on access by Wednesday and successful remaining
> checks. Ada has not accepted a release or an email-scope addition.

A stakeholder update can lead with readiness and consequence:

> The export candidate has passed the recorded small-data filter check, but release
> readiness is still unconfirmed. We are waiting for provider access and have an
> accessibility review remaining. Jo owns access and integration follow-up; Mei owns
> the review. Friday is conditional on access by Wednesday and successful checks,
> so it is not a confirmed delivery date. Ada will decide release acceptance from
> that evidence. Scheduled email remains outside the agreed export scope.

The second message explains the practical limitation without requiring the reader
to know the staging build identifier. It does not conceal the unstarted review
or blame another group. [Google's audience guidance](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/audience)
connects explanation to the reader's task and existing knowledge. Job title alone
does not tell you what they know: a stakeholder may need a build identifier, and
a newly joined engineer may need “integration check” explained.

Neither rewrite invents a next-update time or claims the provider has agreed to
a deadline. In real work, propose a specific update time and obtain or make the
appropriate commitment. Keep the difference between a proposed next action and
an already accepted assignment clear.

### 3) **Resolve a disagreement constructively**

“That's scope creep and unsafe” names conclusions without helping decide. A
teammate reply can say:

> Shipping now would leave the shared-provider and accessibility evidence unresolved.
> Adding scheduled email changes the accepted download-only scope. Please keep
> those decisions separate: Jo and Mei can report the remaining checks, and Ada
> can decide any explicit scope change and release acceptance. What customer evidence
> supports the email request, and what acceptance criteria would it require?

A stakeholder reply can say:

> We can consider scheduled email as a separate product change. The current agreement
> covers downloading the selected workshop's file. Email adds a new delivery outcome
> that has no agreed criteria in this packet. Ada needs to decide that scope with
> the customer evidence; it should not be described as already included or accepted.
> The current candidate also has readiness checks remaining.

Both explain the concern and the decision needed. They avoid guessing whether
the teammate is careless or the customers actually requested email. The earlier
[review lesson](<../5. Documents and decisions/5.2. Review feedback trade-offs assumptions and action items.md>) owns the general method
for linking evidence, consequence and action.

Practice two changes. First, the provider confirms access for Thursday. Revise the
access expectation, but do not call Friday confirmed: the original Wednesday
assumption no longer holds and checks remain. Second, Ada explicitly accepts email
as new scope. Stop calling it unapproved, while asking for its criteria and revised
plan. A scope decision alone neither implements email nor validates E7.

### 4) **Common confusion**

Concise writing is not permission to drop uncertainty. “Almost ready” can hide an
unknown external date and two uncompleted checks. Likewise, respectful disagreement
can be direct: name the conflicting requirement and the needed decision. Polite
phrasing without a clear concern leaves the recipient unable to respond.

### 5) **Performance note**

A status rewrite changes communication, not application speed. E7's filter result
contains no duration or workload measurement. Do not add “fast,” “scalable” or a
performance improvement to either version because one synthetic case passed.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Give the learner only the packet and require two drafts. Assess whether every
assertion has evidence, uncertainty survives, owners remain distinct and each
reader can identify the next decision. Then supply Thursday access or the accepted
email change. A good revision changes dependent claims without rewriting history.
