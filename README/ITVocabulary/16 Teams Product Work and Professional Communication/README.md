# Teams Product Work and Professional Communication

Learn to interpret people, product work and professional communication through explicit responsibilities, requirements and evidence. Build on the reading habits in domain 1 and the preceding technical contexts. Examples are constructed reading exercises; titles and processes depend on the organization.

This domain is complete: all 13 approved lessons are available.

## 1. People and responsibility

### [1.1. Stakeholders users customers and technical roles](<sections/1. People and responsibility/1.1. Stakeholders users customers and technical roles.md>)

1. **Distinguish affected people from buyers and users:** Identify needs interests and decision authority without assuming the payer represents every user.
2. **Interpret role names through actual responsibilities:** Expand PM PO QA SRE TL EM and IC in context and separate framework accountabilities from job titles.
3. **Route a product question to the relevant people:** Read a fictional export discussion and preserve the difference between implementation evidence user need and acceptance authority.

### [1.2. Ownership accountability DRI RACI and escalation](<sections/1. People and responsibility/1.2. Ownership accountability DRI RACI and escalation.md>)

1. **State the responsibility and its authority boundary:** Separate owning an outcome doing work and deciding within a defined scope.
2. **Read a responsibility matrix with its legend:** Interpret DRI and RACI without assuming universal decision rights or treating consultation as approval.
3. **Escalate an unresolved decision with a usable handoff:** Identify the needed decision its current owner and fallback while distinguishing acknowledgment from resolution.

## 2. Requirements and product scope

### [2.1. Requirements constraints acceptance criteria and quality attributes](<sections/2. Requirements and product scope/2.1. Requirements constraints acceptance criteria and quality attributes.md>)

1. **Separate the needed outcome from constraints on the solution:** Read functional requirements quality concerns and imposed boundaries without mistaking a suggestion for an obligation.
2. **Make acceptance evidence observable and scoped:** Connect criteria to user needs conditions and a decision while separating planned checks from observed results.
3. **Repair a vague export requirement without inventing approval:** Classify an original requirement packet identify conflicting conditions and propose a measurable revision for the assigned owner.

### [2.2. Roadmaps backlogs epics stories tasks MVPs and prototypes](<sections/2. Requirements and product scope/2.2. Roadmaps backlogs epics stories tasks MVPs and prototypes.md>)

1. **Distinguish product direction from work decomposition:** Read roadmaps backlogs epics stories and tasks with explicit local hierarchy and commitment status.
2. **Choose a learning artifact for the uncertainty:** Separate prototype proof of concept and MVP purposes from evidence that a deliverable is ready for production.
3. **Read a polished demonstration without overstating progress:** Classify an original workshop follow-up packet and state which user feasibility and operating questions remain.

## 3. Work methods

### [3.1. Agile Scrum Kanban Lean iterations and sprints](<sections/3. Work methods/3.1. Agile Scrum Kanban Lean iterations and sprints.md>)

1. **Separate adaptive values from a named framework:** Interpret Agile and Scrum without equating software feedback with meetings or abandoning documentation.
2. **Distinguish timeboxed learning from managed flow:** Explain iteration increment Sprint Kanban WIP and Lean concerns with explicit framework boundaries.
3. **Evaluate a process claim from observed work:** Read a constructed board and Sprint packet and propose a focused improvement without declaring unmeasured productivity gains.

## 4. Planning and progress

### [4.1. Estimates forecasts deadlines effort capacity and velocity](<sections/4. Planning and progress/4.1. Estimates forecasts deadlines effort capacity and velocity.md>)

1. **State what quantity a planning number describes:** Separate effort elapsed duration capacity estimates forecasts and deadline constraints with explicit units.
2. **Use team history without turning points into a universal clock:** Explain relative story points completed-work velocity and changes that weaken forecasting assumptions.
3. **Challenge a date claim using its actual premises:** Calculate a bounded fictional planning model and preserve dependencies uncertainty and the difference between a forecast and a commitment.

### [4.2. Dependencies blockers scope creep Definition of Done and handoffs](<sections/4. Planning and progress/4.2. Dependencies blockers scope creep Definition of Done and handoffs.md>)

1. **Describe what prevents which outcome:** Separate dependencies current blockers and risks then identify owners and the decisions needed to proceed.
2. **Keep scope and completion criteria visible:** Distinguish agreed scope change from unmanaged expansion and Definition of Done from an activity label or item-specific criterion.
3. **Transfer unfinished work without inventing completion:** Rewrite a constructed implementation-complete update with exact remaining checks scope decisions and a usable receiving-owner handoff.

## 5. Documents and decisions

### [5.1. PRDs RFCs ADRs proposals specifications and decision records](<sections/5. Documents and decisions/5.1. PRDs RFCs ADRs proposals specifications and decision records.md>)

1. **Choose a document from its purpose and authority:** Distinguish product requirements discussion proposals normative specifications and retained decision rationale.
2. **Read decision state separately from implementation state:** Interpret proposed accepted rejected and superseded records without erasing earlier context or inventing deployment evidence.
3. **Reconcile conflicting documents through scope and history:** Trace an original product and architecture packet to its current decision open questions and required follow-up.

### [5.2. Review feedback trade-offs assumptions and action items](<sections/5. Documents and decisions/5.2. Review feedback trade-offs assumptions and action items.md>)

1. **Write feedback that identifies evidence and consequence:** Separate required correction clarification suggestions and preference while making the concern actionable.
2. **Explain an option choice with assumptions and trade-offs:** Compare alternatives against the actual requirements and distinguish observations hypotheses and premises.
3. **Turn disagreement into a decision and verifiable follow-up:** Rewrite a constructed cache review with scoped rationale ownership and completion evidence without claiming unperformed results.

## 8. Applied professional communication

### [8.1. Rewrite a status update and disagreement for two audiences](<sections/8. Applied professional communication/8.1. Rewrite a status update and disagreement for two audiences.md>)

2. **Adapt detail to two audiences:** preserve facts and uncertainty while changing explanation and emphasis
3. **Resolve a disagreement constructively:** connect a concern to its consequence decision owner and follow-up

### [9.1. Boundaries of team product and business vocabulary](<sections/9. Overlaps to avoid/9.1. Boundaries of team product and business vocabulary.md>)
