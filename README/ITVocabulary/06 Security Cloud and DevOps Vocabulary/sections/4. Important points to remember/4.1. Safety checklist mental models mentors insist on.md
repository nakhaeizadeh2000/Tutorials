## 4.1. Safety checklist (mental models mentors insist on)

### 1) **Name the weakness and its score**

CVE + CVSS + reachability — before debating urgency (from [1.1](<../1. Safety words/1.1. Threat vulnerability CVE and patch.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "CVE-2024-1234, CVSS 9.1, internet-facing Jenkins with deploy keys — patch today" — weakness, score, exposure, action. Score without reachability is trivia; reachability without score is panic.
- **Bad habit to break**: patching by headline ("it's in the news!") instead of by score-times-exposure — the news covers drama, your edge covers risk.

### 2) **Name the identity and its scope**

Who + minimum access + expiry — in every access grant (from [1.2](<../1. Safety words/1.2. Auth identity secrets and least privilege.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "deploy-bot: write to prod-deploy role only, 90-day rotation, no human login" — identity, scope, lifetime. Any grant missing one field is future attacker leverage with paperwork.
- **Bad habit to break**: standing admin "just in case" — convenience now, blast radius later; break-glass with expiry covers every legitimate emergency.

### 3) **Name the reversal**

Rollback path + time-to-restore — before every release (from [2.2](<../2. Flow words/2.2. On-call runbook hotfix and rollback words.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "release 2.14.1: flag flipback <2 min, prior artifact staged" — path plus minutes. A release without a timed reversal plan is a hope, not a plan.
- **Bad habit to break**: "we'll figure out rollback if needed" — figuring out under fire is how 10-minute incidents become 10-hour ones.

### 4) **Common confusion**

- "Security checklists slow shipping" — the three names take seconds and prevent the incident classes that halt shipping for days; net velocity strongly positive.
- "Checklist items are obvious" — obvious under calm, invisible under fire — which is exactly when the checklist reads itself aloud.

### 5) **Mentor note**

Attach the triple-name rule to deploy approvals: no release merges its checklist (weakness-score, identity-scope, reversal-time) unchecked. Automation helps — CI gates for secret-scanning, reviewers asking the three questions — but the cultural bar ("we don't ship unnamed risk") is what compounds quarter after quarter.
