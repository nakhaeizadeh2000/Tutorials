## 1.2. Means medians percentiles distributions and tail latency

An average response time of 28 ms can coexist with a request that took 1,000 ms.
A summary compresses observations; different summaries preserve different questions.
Read the population, time window and calculation before comparing two dashboard
numbers.

The preceding lesson owns timing boundaries and qualifying outcomes. This lesson
uses supplied nonnegative integer durations in milliseconds. Its calculation is
executable, but the inputs are synthetic rather than measurements of a service.

### 1) **Distinguish a center from a distribution and its tail**

A **distribution** describes how observations are spread across values. For a
finite sample, ask how many values occur in each range and whether several groups
behave differently. The **arithmetic mean** adds all values and divides by their
count. The word average often means this, but clarify it in an ambiguous report.

The **median** describes the center of sorted observations. For an odd count,
use the middle value. For an even count, a common sample convention averages the
two middle values. NIST's [measures of location](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda351.htm)
compares these calculations and their different sensitivity to extreme values.
A median need not be an observed value: the median of 10 and 30 is 20 under this
convention.

**Tail latency** concerns the high end of response durations. A favorable center
can hide a smaller population of slow requests. Conversely, one maximum does not
show how frequently requests are slow. Report both the relevant summary and enough
population information to understand the question. Do not automatically discard
slow observations as outliers: they may represent the users whose experience
matters. Investigate whether they are valid observations and which workload they
represent.

A **histogram** groups observations into ranges, or buckets, and records counts.
For example, counts in 0–20 ms, above 20–200 ms and above 200 ms reveal more spread
than one mean. Bucket boundaries and included outcomes matter. A histogram is
still a compressed representation; a wide bucket does not retain each exact value.

### 2) **Read a percentile with its calculation and aggregation limits**

A **percentile** locates a value relative to a percentage of an ordered population.
The labels **p50**, **p95** and **p99** refer to the 50th, 95th and 99th percentiles.
A **quantile** uses a fraction instead: the 0.95 quantile corresponds to p95.
High percentiles describe the slow end of a duration sample, not a percentage of
the mean or a probability that every future request meets a target.

Finite-sample calculations vary. NIST's [percentile discussion](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc262.htm)
compares several interpolation conventions; an estimate can fall between observed
values. Here we deliberately choose **nearest rank**: sort n values, calculate
p × n / 100, round the rank upward, then select that one-based position. The code
supports integer p from 1 through 100. This is a transparent classroom convention,
not a claim that every monitoring tool uses it.

For 100 values, this rule selects position 99 for p99. The final value can be larger.
For very small samples, a high percentile can instead select the maximum. Under
nearest rank, p50 of [10, 30] is 10, while the even-sample median convention above
returns 20. Do not treat a difference between these named calculations as a broken
program; identify the rule used by the report.

**Do not average worker percentiles to obtain the service percentile.** The
individual values and their ranks in the combined population are missing. Even
weighting those percentile values by request count cannot reconstruct the missing
information. Merge the underlying observations or an appropriate mergeable
distribution representation for the same intended population and window.

Prometheus documents [histograms and summaries](https://prometheus.io/docs/practices/histograms/)
as different ways to retain distribution information. Precomputed summary quantiles
do not supply a pooled percentile. Histogram aggregation can retain usable counts,
but its percentile estimate depends on the representation and interpolation.
Current documentation distinguishes classic and native histograms; actual support
and aggregation rules must match the chosen library and backend. This vocabulary
lesson does not prescribe a configuration or claim that every bucket layout merges.

Means have a different aggregation rule: sum the values and divide by total count.
For groups [10, 10] and [100], the combined mean is 120/3 = 40, not the unweighted
average of group means, 55. Keep group sums and counts for that calculation. The
rule for means does not make an average of percentiles valid.

### 3) **Calculate a sample and expose a misleading worker summary**

Save `duration_summaries.py` and run `python3 duration_summaries.py`. Tested on
**CPython 3.14.4, Linux x86_64**, standard library only. The lists contain durations,
not repeated calls to a service. Sorting creates an ordered copy. Integer arithmetic
computes the upward-rounded rank without a floating-point boundary calculation.

```python
from statistics import median


def nearest_rank(values, percentile):
    if not values or type(percentile) is not int or not 1 <= percentile <= 100:
        raise ValueError("need observations and an integer percentile from 1 to 100")
    ordered = sorted(values)
    rank = (percentile * len(ordered) + 99) // 100
    return ordered[rank - 1]


sample = [10] * 90 + [100] * 9 + [1000]
print("Mean ms:", sum(sample) / len(sample))
print("Median ms:", median(sample))
print("p95 ms:", nearest_rank(sample, 95))
print("p99 ms:", nearest_rank(sample, 99))
print("Maximum ms:", max(sample))

worker_a = [10] * 99 + [1000]
worker_b = [1000] * 100
worker_p99s = [nearest_rank(worker_a, 99), nearest_rank(worker_b, 99)]
print("Worker p99s ms:", worker_p99s)
print("Incorrect averaged p99 ms:", sum(worker_p99s) / len(worker_p99s))
print("Pooled p99 ms:", nearest_rank(worker_a + worker_b, 99))
print("Small-sample p50 ms:", nearest_rank([10, 30], 50))
print("Small-sample median ms:", median([10, 30]))
```

Observed calculation output:

```text
Mean ms: 28.0
Median ms: 10.0
p95 ms: 100
p99 ms: 100
Maximum ms: 1000
Worker p99s ms: [10, 1000]
Incorrect averaged p99 ms: 505.0
Pooled p99 ms: 1000
Small-sample p50 ms: 10
Small-sample median ms: 20.0
```

In the first sample, 90 requests take 10 ms, 9 take 100 ms and 1 takes 1,000 ms.
The mean is 2,800/100 = 28 ms. Positions 95 and 99 are both 100 ms, while position
100 is 1,000 ms. The p99 is therefore not the maximum and does not promise a 100 ms
upper bound for every observation.

The workers contribute equal counts, so request-count weighting would still give
505 ms. In the combined 200-value sample, 99 values are 10 ms and 101 are 1,000 ms;
position 198 is 1,000 ms. The direct calculation disproves averaging as a general
pooling rule without relying on a dashboard implementation.

Practice: remove the slowest observation from the first sample because the report
collects only requests completed before a deadline. The 1,000 ms observation is now
absent from that report, rather than improved. Document the exclusion and track
timeouts or unfinished work appropriately. A population change can improve a
summary while leaving the actual user outcome unchanged.

### 4) **Common confusion**

A p99 of 100 ms is not “99% of requests take exactly 100 ms” and not a guarantee
for future traffic. For this sample and nearest-rank rule, 99 of the 100 values
are at or below 100 ms. That statement remains scoped to those inputs.

More samples do not repair an incorrect timing boundary or exclusion rule. Different
routes, successes and failures can also produce different distributions. Compare
like populations before interpreting a change as a regression or improvement.

### 5) **Performance note**

This teaching function sorts n observations, conventionally requiring O(n log n)
comparison work and O(n) space for its sorted copy. It demonstrates exact ranks,
not a recommended production telemetry implementation. Streaming representations
trade retained detail, resource cost and estimation error. No timing or memory
benchmark of this function, histogram system or application was run.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask why 100 ms p99 and 1,000 ms maximum can both be correct, then ask the learner
to locate position 198 in the pooled worker sample. A sound answer reasons from
the counts and rank rule, rejects 505 ms as a pooled result and asks whether the
report excluded unfinished requests. Use [10, 30] to expose an assumed universal
agreement between median and every p50 implementation.
