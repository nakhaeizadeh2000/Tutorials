## 5.1. Availability reliability durability resilience and fault tolerance

A service can preserve all stored records while nobody can reach it. It can also
respond promptly after losing a record. “Reliable storage” and “the service is up”
therefore need an operation, a property and a failure scope rather than one shared
interpretation of reliable.

The earlier data, security and distributed-systems domains own their specific
guarantees. This lesson compares service properties through fictional evidence;
it performs no failure or recovery experiment.

### 1) **Identify the property and the service boundary**

**Availability** concerns being able to provide the required service when needed.
For a measurement, define what available means, for whom, over which interval or
request population. A process answering a health request does not establish that
a registration operation completes correctly through its database dependency.

**Reliability** often concerns delivering correct service consistently over the
required operation or period. In formal dependability terminology, continuity of
correct service is distinguished from readiness to provide it. The original
[Avižienis, Laprie, Randell and Landwehr taxonomy](https://www.landwehr.org/2004-aviz-laprie-randell.pdf)
uses that distinction. In everyday engineering, reliability can be a broader label
including availability and recovery. Identify the author's definition instead of
forcing every team to reserve the words for mutually exclusive categories.

**Durability** concerns retaining accepted data under stated storage and failure
assumptions. The earlier transaction and copy lessons own this property. Retained
data can be temporarily inaccessible; a live service can have lost previously
accepted information. A durability claim must specify the acknowledged operation,
retention scope and failures covered.

AWS's primary [availability discussion](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/reliability-pillar/availability.html)
provides both time-based and request-based models. Keep their denominators and
conditions distinct, as the SLO lesson explains. Do not transfer a component's
published target directly to the complete user workflow: dependencies and the
measurement boundary matter.

| Question | Property or evidence to examine |
|---|---|
| Can an eligible user complete registration now? | Availability for that operation |
| Did the required service continue correctly through the stated period? | Reliability under the specified definition |
| Does an acknowledged registration remain recoverable after the specified storage fault? | Durability under that fault and storage contract |
| Does a health endpoint answer? | That endpoint's observation, which may be narrower than all three |

### 2) **Distinguish continuing through faults from recovering and adapting**

**Fault tolerance** is the ability to continue the required service despite faults
within a defined model. Specify the fault and acceptable service: a test involving
one application host does not establish tolerance of a region outage, shared data
corruption or a faulty update installed on every instance. The dependability taxonomy
links fault tolerance to avoiding service failures in the presence of faults.

**Resilience**, also written **resiliency**, concerns withstanding disruption,
recovering and adapting to preserve or restore the required outcomes. It can
include controlled degradation and recovery after an interruption. In a scoped
cyber context, NIST's [cyber resiliency definition](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/cyber_resiliency)
includes anticipation, withstanding, recovery and adaptation. AWS's [resiliency discussion](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/reliability-pillar/resiliency-and-the-components-of-reliability.html)
applies a related model to workload disruptions and changing demand. These are
contextual definitions, not promises that adding one mechanism covers every fault.

**Graceful degradation** preserves selected useful functions when others cannot
operate normally. Suppose optional recommendations disappear while registration
still meets its requirements. That can be an intentional degraded mode. If the
only useful function is registration and registration fails, displaying an error
page is not proof that the required function was preserved. State which outcomes
remain and what the product allows to degrade.

Recovery after 30 seconds can demonstrate a recovery capability while still
violating a requirement for uninterrupted service. Conversely, tolerating one
fault without an observed interruption does not establish recovery from lost data
or adaptation to every future overload. Define the objective and failure conditions
before assigning either label.

Operational averages also need their own definitions. **MTBF** commonly means
mean time between failures for the stated component and accounting model. **MTTR**
can mean mean time to repair, recover, restore, respond or resolve under local
conventions. Atlassian's [incident-metric discussion](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/kpis/common-metrics)
illustrates why the expansion and timing boundaries must be stated.
Record the start/end events and included incidents. An average alone does not show
how long every failure lasted or how failures cluster. Do not infer an exact user
availability percentage from two unexplained acronyms.

### 3) **Classify failure evidence without expanding its scope**

Read these **independent constructed arrangements**:

| Arrangement | Supplied observation | Supported reading |
|---|---|---|
| A | Stored registrations remain intact after a storage component outage, but registration requests cannot complete for ten minutes | Preservation evidence for the stated data; an availability interruption for registration |
| B | A responding service restarts after a disk fault, but a previously acknowledged registration has no recoverable copy | Responsiveness does not establish that record's durability |
| C | In a controlled scenario, one application host is removed; all 100 checked registrations still return correct results within the declared 500 ms requirement | Fault-tolerance evidence for that host fault and those checked operations |
| D | All registration requests fail for 30 seconds during a disruption; a recovery procedure then restores the required operation | Recovery evidence with an interruption, not uninterrupted registration in that scenario |
| E | Recommendations are disabled during overload while all checked registrations meet their requirements | Evidence for the stated degraded mode; not proof that every feature stayed available |

A's surviving data does not make the unavailable registration path available.
B's live process does not repair the missing record. C is stronger than a picture
of two boxes: it supplies scoped behavior under a fault. It still does not establish
zero failures for every input, future period or different failure type. D and E
show why recovery and preserving selected outcomes need explicit descriptions.

Practice: change C's requirement to correct registration within 100 ms, and add
that its 100 checked response times range from 150 to 400 ms. The same observed
responses now fail the timing requirement. Under this service definition, saying
the scenario preserved the required service would be wrong. The mechanism and
physical fault are unchanged; the acceptable outcome changed.

A useful handoff names the operation, fault, observed interruption or loss, recovery
result and untested scope. “Fault tolerant, therefore durable and always available”
skips those distinctions and gives the next reader no way to assess the claim.

### 4) **Common confusion**

Replication, backup and additional instances are mechanisms. Availability,
durability and continued correct service are properties evaluated under conditions.
The names of the mechanisms do not establish all properties, especially when copies
share a failure boundary or propagate the same unwanted change.

An availability figure also does not describe outage frequency and duration by
itself. Several short interruptions and one long interruption can have the same
total unavailable time while affecting users differently. Preserve relevant history
instead of treating one percentage as the complete reliability story.

### 5) **Performance note**

A response that exceeds the required latency can fail the defined service objective
even when its content is correct. Redundancy, replication and recovery procedures
can add coordination, storage and capacity costs; they need workload and failure
analysis rather than a universal cost multiplier. No failure rate, recovery time,
reliability probability, data durability or performance property was measured here.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask whether A lost data, whether it maintained registration availability, and
whether B's health response proves recovery. Then change C's timing requirement.
A sound explanation keeps the same observations while revising the property claim
under the new service definition. Ask the learner to state the missing fault model
before accepting an unqualified fault-tolerant label.
