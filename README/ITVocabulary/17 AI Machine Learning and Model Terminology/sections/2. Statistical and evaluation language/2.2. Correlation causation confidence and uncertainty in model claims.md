## 2.2. Correlation causation confidence and uncertainty in model claims

“Teams using our AI close tickets faster, so AI causes faster support.” The
observation may be real while the causal conclusion remains unsupported. Predicting
an outcome from existing differences and changing that outcome through an intervention
are different questions.

### 1) **Separate prediction from intervention**

**Association** means measured variables vary together; **correlation** commonly
summarizes such association, often with a particular numerical measure. A **causal
claim** says an intervention changes an outcome relative to a specified alternative.
“Enable suggestions rather than disable them” is a clearer intervention than
“use more AI.” Define the people, task, outcome and period too.

**Confounding** occurs when other factors distort the proposed cause-and-effect
comparison. Experienced staff may both adopt suggestions earlier and resolve cases
faster. Case difficulty, staffing or product changes may also differ. A predictive
relationship can be useful without showing that forcing adoption will produce
the same outcome.

Random assignment to defined conditions can help isolate an intervention's effect
by reducing systematic group differences. It does not guarantee identical groups
in a finite sample or solve missing outcomes, changed behavior and poor measurement.
[Berkeley's experimental-design notes](https://stat20.berkeley.edu/spring-2025/4-causation/02-experiments/notes.html)
explain control and assignment. Observational causal methods also exist, but their
identification assumptions require specialist analysis. This vocabulary lesson
neither prescribes an experiment on real users nor teaches that analysis.

### 2) **Interpret uncertainty in context**

A **model confidence score** might be a predicted probability, transformed score
or vendor-specific indicator. A value of 0.9 is not automatically evidence that
90% of similar deployed decisions are correct. Ask how it was produced and assessed.
**Calibration** concerns agreement between predicted probabilities and observed
frequencies over an appropriate population. A model can rank cases usefully while
its probability estimates remain poorly calibrated.

A **confidence interval** is a different statistical object: an interval estimate
for a population quantity under a stated method and assumptions. For a conventional
95% procedure, repeated samples and interval construction would cover the fixed
quantity about 95% of the time when the assumptions hold. It is not an interval
containing 95% of individual outcomes, and it is not a model's self-reported confidence.
[NIST's explanation](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section1/prc14.htm)
gives that repeated-sampling interpretation. Under the conventional fixed-parameter
interpretation, the reported level does not assign a 95% probability to the fixed
parameter lying inside this one realized interval.

**Uncertainty** also includes what an interval may omit: unsuitable labels, missing
populations, changed traffic, unknown dependencies and disagreement about the task.
More decimal places do not repair those gaps. A narrow interval from a flawed
sampling design can describe the wrong quantity very precisely.

### 3) **Rewrite a causal capability claim**

Use this invented proposal packet; these are supplied observations and reported
statistics, not an experiment or interval calculated in this lesson.

| Item | Supplied information |
|---|---|
| Group A | Volunteers using suggestions; average recorded handling time 6 minutes |
| Group B | Nonvolunteers; average recorded handling time 10 minutes |
| Assignment | No random assignment; volunteers include more experienced staff |
| Measurement | Only closed tickets included; case difficulty and missing cases not assessed |
| Reported interval | Analyst reports 95% interval of 2–6 minutes for the observed group difference under an unspecified method |
| Model output | “Confidence 0.95” attached to one suggested route; no calibration report |
| Summary | “AI saves every person four minutes with 95% certainty” |

The observed mean difference is four minutes in the included closed tickets. It
does not establish the intervention's effect, much less every individual's saving.
Experience and selection can explain part or all of the difference; their actual
effect remains unmeasured. Excluding unclosed cases can change which work the
comparison represents.

The reported interval needs its target quantity, assumptions and construction
method. It does not transform an observational difference into a causal effect.
The route's confidence number describes another object altogether and cannot
validate the handling-time claim.

A defensible rewrite is: “Among included closed tickets, volunteers averaged six
minutes versus ten for nonvolunteers. Groups differed in experience and were not
randomly assigned, so the four-minute difference does not establish a causal saving.
The reported interval method and route-score calibration remain unverified.”

Now supply credible random assignment while retaining excluded unclosed cases.
That improves the assignment basis but leaves outcome selection and measurement
questions. Alternatively, supply a calibration study for routing. That addresses
route probabilities in its assessed population, not the cause of handling-time
differences. New evidence should resolve the question it actually measures.

### 4) **Common confusion**

Statistical confidence, conversational certainty and model scores share a word,
not an interpretation. Likewise, “correlation is not causation” does not mean
causation can never be studied or associations are useless. Name the design and
the assumptions needed to move from observation to intervention.

### 5) **Performance note**

Handling time, inference latency and end-to-end service time are different measures.
A shorter mean for closed tickets does not show a faster model or better completion
for all incoming cases. This packet contains no runtime benchmark or verified
causal performance effect.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask the learner which object each 95% number describes. Then add random assignment
alone or calibration alone and require a revised conclusion. A good answer credits
the added evidence while retaining the remaining selection and measurement limits.
