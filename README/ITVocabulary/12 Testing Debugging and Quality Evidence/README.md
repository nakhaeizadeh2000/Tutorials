# Testing Debugging and Quality Evidence

Read quality claims by identifying expectations, observations and the boundaries actually exercised. Build on the earlier program, API and release vocabulary. Small controlled examples distinguish failure evidence from hypotheses; full automation methodology and language-specific debugging remain with their detailed owners.

This domain is in progress: 8 of 10 approved lessons are available.

## 1. Faults and evidence

### [1.1. Errors defects bugs failures exceptions and regressions](<sections/1. Faults and evidence/1.1. Errors defects bugs failures exceptions and regressions.md>)

1. **Separate a mistake from a defect and an observed failure:** Interpret testing conventions and team variation without inferring a unique cause or human mistake from a failed operation.
2. **Observe a wrong result and a correctly handled exception separately:** Run a small report comparison and distinguish a latent selection defect, an observed wrong result and required invalid-input handling.
3. **Identify a regression from a change and a preserved expectation:** Compare earlier and changed behavior, revise claims when history or requirements change, and explain what the limited correction check establishes.

### [1.2. Verification validation QA QC and acceptance](<sections/1. Faults and evidence/1.2. Verification validation QA QC and acceptance.md>)

1. **Identify the reference behind verification and validation:** Separate conformance to specified requirements from intended-use evaluation and the narrower input-validation meaning.
2. **Separate quality activities from department names and acceptance decisions:** Interpret QA, QC, acceptance criteria, acceptance testing and UAT without treating a team name or decision as proof of every result.
3. **Read a conformance result and an unmet need together:** Diagnose a constructed requirements-to-purpose gap and distinguish process improvement, product evaluation and acceptance evidence.

## 2. Test scope and intent

### [2.1. Unit component integration system end-to-end and contract tests](<sections/2. Test scope and intent/2.1. Unit component integration system end-to-end and contract tests.md>)

1. **Define the tested boundary before assigning a level name:** Interpret unit, component, integration, system and end-to-end naming with explicit scope and local conventions.
2. **Trace which real collaborations each arrangement exercises:** Compare five constructed calculation, database, API and browser arrangements and identify the evidence removed by substitutions.
3. **Distinguish interface-contract evidence from a complete workflow:** Explain consumer and provider contract checks, relevant version pairing and the application or deployed-path behavior they do not establish.

### [2.2. Functional non-functional regression smoke and exploratory testing](<sections/2. Test scope and intent/2.2. Functional non-functional regression smoke and exploratory testing.md>)

1. **Separate the tested scope from the quality question and execution method:** Interpret functional and non-functional purposes across levels and distinguish a case, suite and actual run.
2. **Distinguish change protection from a preliminary smoke check:** Compare regression, confirmation and smoke purposes without treating overlapping labels or planned cases as independent passing executions.
3. **Use exploration to turn observations into the next useful experiment:** Read a focused charter and filter investigation, preserve evidence versus hypotheses, and connect exploration with repeatable regression cases.

## 3. Test arrangements and limits

### [3.1. Assertions fixtures test doubles mocks stubs spies and fakes](<sections/3. Test arrangements and limits/3.1. Assertions fixtures test doubles mocks stubs spies and fakes.md>)

1. **Identify the prepared situation and the condition being checked:** Distinguish test assertions, fixture data or resources, setup and cleanup, and the Arrange–Act–Assert reasoning sequence.
2. **Choose a substitute by its role rather than its library name:** Interpret dummy, stub, spy, mock and fake roles and the real behavior each arrangement leaves unverified.
3. **Run a recording collaborator and test what it establishes:** Execute a local interaction check, observe a wrong-total failure and separate the prepared receipt and recorded call from real delivery.

### [3.2. Coverage flaky tests false positives and false negatives](<sections/3. Test arrangements and limits/3.2. Coverage flaky tests false positives and false negatives.md>)

1. **Read coverage as a scoped execution measurement:** Interpret statement line branch and function criteria, identify denominator changes and distinguish execution from requirements and assertion quality.
2. **Define the positive signal before classifying a mistaken result:** Compare defect signals with actual behavior and an independent expectation, including the role and limits of a test oracle.
3. **Interpret intermittent outcomes without erasing the first failure:** Read a retry history, separate flaky outcomes from mistaken signals and judge investigation and quarantine boundaries.

## 4. Investigation language

### [4.1. Reproduction hypotheses stack traces breakpoints and root causes](<sections/4. Investigation language/4.1. Reproduction hypotheses stack traces breakpoints and root causes.md>)

1. **Separate a reproducible symptom from a proposed explanation:** Describe reproduction conditions and choose an observation that can distinguish hypotheses instead of assuming a cause.
2. **Read a traceback and inspect a paused frame:** Run a deliberate local failure and actual debugger commands, interpret call context and distinguish inspection from correction.
3. **Connect a correction to causal evidence and its limits:** Check the original input and a contrasting valid case, scope a root-cause claim and distinguish workaround mitigation and correction.

## 5. Applied reporting and review

### [5.1. Write a reproducible issue with expected and actual behavior](<sections/5. Applied reporting and review/5.1. Write a reproducible issue with expected and actual behavior.md>)

1. **Turn a complaint into a reproducible comparison:** Identify the trigger environment initial state and reference for the expectation without assuming a diagnosis.
2. **Write a complete report from a bounded evidence packet:** Produce a useful issue with precise steps observed outcomes scoped frequency and explicit unknowns.
3. **Refine the report when evidence or expectations change:** Revise missing-rule and intermittent variants,choose useful attachments and preserve the difference between observed and proposed reproductions.
