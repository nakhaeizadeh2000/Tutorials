## 1.2. Verification validation QA QC and acceptance

A team proves that an export contains exactly the requested columns. Staff still
cannot use it to do their work because the request omitted the records they need.
The export can satisfy a specification while leaving an important user need
unmet. These are different quality questions, not contradictory observations.

Read the previous defect and failure lesson first. This page compares evaluation
purposes and responsibilities through constructed records; it does not require
a test framework or claim an actual product evaluation.

### 1) **Identify the reference behind verification and validation**

**Verification** checks conformance to specified requirements. State which
requirements, product version and conditions the evidence covers. Reviewing a
calculation against its specified rule and executing a check of that rule can
both contribute to verification. The word does not mean “automated test” or
“mathematical proof of the whole product.”

**Validation** evaluates whether the product meets its intended purpose and users'
needs in the intended context. A product can meet a written requirement that was
incomplete or inappropriate for that purpose. Validation can expose the gap before
or after implementation; it is not merely a final approval meeting.

NASA's [verification and validation distinction](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/2-0-fundamentals-of-systems-engineering/#h-2-4-distinctions-between-product-verification-and-product-validation)
separates requirements conformance from purpose in the intended environment. It
also describes several possible evidence methods, including inspection, analysis,
demonstration and testing. This lesson uses that purpose distinction without
requiring NASA's organizational process. The
[ISTQB CTFL 4.0.1 syllabus](https://istqb.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ISTQB_CTFL_Syllabus_v4.0.1.pdf),
section 1.1, likewise includes both purposes in testing.

Avoid assigning each activity one permanent label without stating its question.
An observed export workflow might check specified columns and also investigate
whether staff can complete their task. The evidence contributes to each claim
only within what it actually covered. Neither word establishes that all possible
inputs or environments were evaluated.

Software developers also use **validation** to mean checking incoming data against
a schema or rule. That narrower mechanism can support a product requirement, but
accepting a JSON shape does not evaluate whether the whole product serves its
users. The earlier schema lesson owns that input-checking meaning.

### 2) **Separate quality activities from department names and acceptance decisions**

**Quality assurance**, or **QA**, concerns planned activities that provide confidence
in quality, including how work is performed and improved. **Quality control**, or
**QC**, concerns activities that evaluate and control product or service quality
against the relevant requirements. They are related parts of quality management;
they are not a reliable division into “manual people” and “automated scripts.”

For example, investigating why requirements repeatedly omit operational users,
then improving the requirements-review process, serves QA. Inspecting this release's
export against its stated rules serves QC. Results from that inspection can also
inform the process improvement. The [ASQ comparison](https://asq.org/quality-resources/quality-assurance-vs-control)
explains their connected roles and acknowledges variation in usage. A team called
“QA” may perform testing, process improvement or both. Ask what work it actually
does and who owns the release decision.

**Acceptance** is a decision that a deliverable is acceptable for a stated purpose
under the relevant agreement or policy. **Acceptance criteria** are conditions used
to judge that outcome. **Acceptance testing** gathers evidence focused on the
relevant needs and readiness; **user acceptance testing**, or **UAT**, focuses on
acceptance from intended users' perspective. Acceptance work may also concern
operational, contractual or other requirements. The detailed test-boundary comparison
comes later in this domain.

A passing result and an acceptance decision are different records. A stakeholder
can accept a scoped deliverable with known limitations if the agreement permits
it, but that decision does not make the limitations disappear or turn unexecuted
checks into passing results. Conversely, failing to meet an acceptance criterion
requires a decision under that agreement, not an assumption that someone silently
waived it.

Michael Bolton's original [Verify and Challenge](https://developsense.com/blog/2017/03/deeper-testing-1-verify-and-challenge)
argues for investigating limits beyond a prescribed successful path. That is a
useful testing habit here. It does not mean abandoning explicit requirements:
challenge their adequacy as well as checking the product against them.

### 3) **Read a conformance result and an unmet need together**

This fictional project helps workshop coordinators contact people whose bookings
are still pending. Read the records without treating them as an actual test run.

| Record | Supplied information |
|---|---|
| Intended task | Coordinators must identify pending bookings and contact those people before the workshop |
| Written export requirement | Produce a CSV containing approved bookings only, with booking ID and contact address |
| Export evaluation | For the supplied data, the produced file has the specified columns and exactly the approved records |
| Task walkthrough | Pending bookings are absent from the export, and this workflow provides no other pending-booking list |
| Acceptance note | The task owner has not accepted the feature for the follow-up task |

The export evaluation supports conformance to the stated selection and column
requirements for its supplied data. It does not establish every CSV case, large
input or user workflow. The walkthrough reveals that the specified export cannot
supply the pending list needed for the stated task. This is a requirements-to-purpose
gap; it is not proof that the exporter incorrectly implemented “approved only.”

A useful response is: “The checked export matches the current approved-only rule,
but the follow-up task requires pending bookings. Please reconcile that requirement
with the task owner and agree the corresponding acceptance criteria.” Merely
renaming the result “validated” or asking someone to approve the same record
would not answer the missing-purpose question.

A QA response could investigate why the task and selection rule diverged and
improve the review step that should surface such differences. A QC response
could evaluate a revised export against its revised rules. The acceptance owner
then needs the relevant evidence and agreed scope. These can involve the same
people; the terms identify purposes rather than separate mandatory departments.

Practice: add a second, verified pending-booking view that coordinators can use
for the stated workflow, while leaving the approved-only export unchanged. Does
the old export have to be defective? No. The broader workflow may now meet the
need through a separate capability. Evaluate that complete task and its acceptance
criteria. The original export check remains evidence for its own selection rule,
not proof of the newly added view. If the packet merely proposes the new view,
its usefulness remains a hypothesis until the relevant evaluation occurs.

### 4) **Common confusion**

Verification is not inherently static, and validation is not inherently manual.
The objective determines which claim an activity addresses; its method determines
what evidence it supplies. A review can reveal an unsuitable requirement before
code exists, and a running system can be evaluated against explicit requirements.

“QA passed” is incomplete without the evaluated object, criteria and result.
A department name is not evidence. Likewise, acceptance does not establish that
all defects are absent. Keep the exact accepted scope, known limitations and
unperformed work attached to the decision.

### 5) **Performance note**

These labels do not directly change program speed. They help distinguish a
performance requirement from actual user need and the evidence for each. An export
could meet a specified generation-time bound while being unusable for a workflow
that needs immediate access to different records. The packet contains no duration
measurements. A performance acceptance criterion needs an operation, workload,
environment, threshold and observation method; the word “fast” supplies none of
them.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Ask the learner which packet row supports each conclusion and which statement
they would revise first: the implemented filter or the requirement. A sound answer
preserves the successful conformance observation and investigates the intended
workflow before proposing a change. Then present the alternative pending view as
first a proposal and then an evaluated capability. The explanation should become
more confident only when the evidence changes, while keeping acceptance authority
and the result of individual checks separate.
