## 5.1. DevOps SRE platform engineering DevSecOps and GitOps

A company creates a “DevOps team,” buys a deployment tool and declares its service
reliable. The team name and tool inventory do not establish how people cooperate,
which operational work is engineered or whether the service meets its users'
needs. Read the practice and its evidence before interpreting the label.

This lesson builds on automation, security, platform and Git vocabulary. Its
work records are fictional; no team reorganization, tool installation or production
change is required. Source-specific definitions are identified explicitly.

### 1) **Distinguish shared delivery practices from reliability engineering**

**DevOps** combines development and operations in a family of practices and
cultural approaches to building, delivering and operating software. Useful
questions include how teams share information, handle operational feedback and
coordinate changes. Automation supports that work, but buying an automation tool
does not itself establish cooperation or clear responsibilities.

DORA's [organizational-culture guidance](https://dora.dev/capabilities/generative-organizational-culture/)
discusses information flow and cooperation across functions. It supplies questions
about working behavior; its research is not proof that changing a job title
causes a particular delivery result. A separate team named DevOps can exist under
an organization's conventions. Inspect what it does and how it interacts with
others rather than declaring the name inherently correct or invalid.

**Site Reliability Engineering**, or **SRE**, applies software-engineering methods
to running and improving services. Google's original
[SRE introduction](https://sre.google/sre-book/introduction/)
explains its approach to engineering operational systems rather than relying
only on repeated manual intervention. Reliability goals, automation, operational
response and feedback into design are relevant activities. Google's historical
staffing ratios and work-allocation rules are its documented practices, not
universal hiring or team-size requirements.

For example, repeatedly restarting an exporter after the same failure is
operational activity. Investigating the failure and engineering a bounded recovery
or correction can improve how the service is operated. The useful question is
which requirement the change addresses and what evidence shows it helps. An SRE
title, a restart script or the word self-healing proves none of those outcomes.

A **service-level objective**, or **SLO**, is a stated service target over a defined
measurement scope and window. It gives reliability work a concrete aim rather
than “make it perfect.” Detailed indicators, targets and budgets are taught in
the later reliability domain. Here, recognize why an SRE discussion needs the
actual target and observed service behavior.

DevOps and SRE can overlap. One describes a broad delivery/operations approach;
the other emphasizes engineering service reliability and operation. Neither
requires every organization to adopt Google's exact structure, nor does one
label prove the absence of the other's practices.

### 2) **Identify platform products security integration and GitOps control loops**

**Platform engineering** develops and evolves shared capabilities for internal
users, such as application teams. An **internal developer platform** combines
interfaces, services, documentation and support into an offering those teams can
use. A **self-service** capability lets an authorized user obtain or operate the
supported capability through its interface with minimal manual intervention.
It does not mean unlimited permissions or no platform maintenance.

The CNCF [platforms white paper](https://tag-app-delivery.cncf.io/whitepapers/platforms/)
emphasizes user needs and the platform as an internal product. A developer portal
can be one interface; it is not the entire platform. Kubernetes can underpin an
offering without itself proving that the users can complete their intended tasks.
A useful **golden path**, also called a **paved road**, supplies a supported route
for a common task, for example a documented application template and delivery
workflow. It still needs limits, ownership and a way to handle unsupported cases.

Evan Bottcher's original [platform discussion](https://martinfowler.com/articles/talk-about-platforms.html)
shows why reusable capabilities and the operating model matter together. Avoid
calling a request form fully self-service if every normal request still requires
an engineer to copy commands manually. Measure the actual user task, including
waiting and failures, before claiming the platform removed that burden.

**DevSecOps** brings security concerns into development, delivery and operation.
NIST's [DevSecOps implementation publication](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/204/c/final)
uses workflows with security, automation and feedback across those activities.
Its microservices/service-mesh context is one implementation scope, not a rule
that every application needs microservices or a mesh to integrate security.

A dependency check can support DevSecOps, but a green scan alone does not prove
correct authorization or incident readiness. Define the checks, response to
findings and retained responsibilities. Adding a final approval column without
usable security work throughout the relevant lifecycle does not establish the
whole practice.

**GitOps** applies a particular model to managing desired system state. Under
[OpenGitOps principles 1.0.0](https://opengitops.dev/), the state is expressed
declaratively, stored with immutable version history, pulled automatically by
software agents and continuously reconciled against actual state. Git commonly
supplies the versioned source. The principles describe behavior rather than
require one named controller product.

**Declarative** means the representation states the intended result, such as
three application replicas, rather than only a sequence of manual commands.
The agent attempts to bring actual state toward that desired state. Permissions,
invalid configuration or unavailable resources can prevent convergence. A desired
state commit is therefore different from a verified operating result.

Storing a deployment script in Git is useful version control, but that fact alone
does not demonstrate automatic pulling or continuous reconciliation. An occasional
script that pushes a change to a remote machine is not evidence of those mechanisms.
GitOps, security integration and platform engineering can work together; they
are not synonyms or mutually exclusive departments.

### 3) **Classify work records and ask for the missing outcome**

Consider these **independent fictional records**:

| Record | Stated work | Supported interpretation | Missing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Application and operations engineers jointly review failed deployments, share observations and change the workflow | DevOps-style cooperation and feedback | Whether delivery outcomes improved under a stated comparison |
| B | Engineers replace a repeated manual recovery task with a tested recovery mechanism tied to a service target | SRE-style operational engineering | Sustained service results beyond the checked recovery cases |
| C | Authorized teams create supported test environments through a documented API; the platform team maintains it and collects user feedback | Platform engineering with a supplied self-service capability | Whether all intended user tasks or exceptional requests are supported |
| D | Security and application engineers define resource-access requirements, check them during changes and track operational findings | DevSecOps-style integration | Whether every relevant threat or control is covered |
| E | An agent pulls versioned desired replica declarations and continuously compares actual state, requesting corrections | GitOps under the stated principles | Whether the latest declaration converged and the application works |

These rows can describe practices inside one organization, but the packet does
not establish a single mandatory organization chart. The evidence concerns work,
interfaces and mechanisms, not a certification earned by adopting the names.

Change only E: retain Git history, but replace the agent with a person manually
running a push script once per release. Version control remains. Automatic pulling
and continuous reconciliation are no longer supplied, so the same GitOps conclusion
under the four stated principles is no longer established. This does not mean the
manual process is automatically defective; it means its mechanisms differ.

Practice: a portal accepts an environment request, creates a ticket and waits for
an engineer to provision the environment manually. Is the complete environment
capability demonstrated as self-service? No. The request intake is automated, but
the requested capability still depends on the supplied manual action. State that
boundary and investigate the users' actual waiting and support needs.

### 4) **Common confusion**

DevOps is not the name of one toolchain, SRE is not a synonym for whoever receives
alerts, and platform engineering is not merely installing Kubernetes or a portal.
DevSecOps is broader than a scanner. GitOps is narrower than every use of Git in
operations. Each label needs its own work and mechanism evidence.

“Everyone shares responsibility” should also lead to specific action ownership.
Shared participation does not tell the reader who investigates a failed rollout,
maintains a template or responds to an ignored access finding. Roles and escalation
can vary without making those duties disappear.

### 5) **Performance note**

Team labels do not directly change application execution speed. Platforms and
automation can change waiting, repeated manual work and delivery effort, while
their agents and supporting services consume resources and require maintenance.
Evaluate those outcomes with the relevant task and service measures. No team
productivity, delivery-speed, reliability, controller-cost or runtime benchmark
was performed for these records.

### 6) **Mentor note**

Give the learner a tool list without team names and ask what working practices
it establishes. Then add the A–E records and ask which conclusions become supported.
A strong answer identifies actions and limits, permits overlapping practices and
asks who owns the missing outcome. Use the manual-script variation to distinguish
version history from a reconciliation mechanism without disparaging a simpler process.
