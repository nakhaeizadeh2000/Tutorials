## 1.1. Sprint, backlog, and velocity

### 1) **Sprint is a time-boxed bet**

**Definition** — A *sprint* (iteration) is a fixed-length work period (usually 1–2 weeks) with a committed goal. *Time-boxing* means the end date never moves — scope flexes, the date doesn't. *Sprint planning* selects the bet; the *sprint goal* states it in one sentence.

- **What problem it solves**: unbounded work expands to fill all time ("it'll be done when it's done" — it won't). Time-boxes convert open-ended effort into discrete, reviewable bets: every two weeks the team holds either done work or a diagnosed miss — both are information, and information compounds.
- **Modern guidance** — protect the box: mid-sprint scope injections go to the backlog, not the sprint (emergencies excepted, and emergencies get declared, not smuggled). A sprint whose scope changes daily is a to-do list wearing agile's clothes — the time-box is the entire methodology.

```text
# Bad: box-breaking (the date absorbs every interruption)
"Sprint 12: original 8 tickets + 5 'quick' stakeholder asks
 added mid-week. Result: 4 done, 9 carried, 0 diagnosed."

# Good: box-protected (interruptions queued, misses diagnosed)
"Sprint 12: 8 tickets committed, 6 done. 2 carried (auth
 scope underestimated — noted for estimation review).
 3 mid-sprint asks → backlog, prioritized Monday."
```

### 2) **Backlog is ordered options**

The (product) *backlog* is the prioritized queue of future work — *user stories* (user-shaped requirements), *epics* (story groups), *spikes* (time-boxed investigations). *Refinement* (grooming) keeps top items small, clear, and estimated; everything below the line stays deliberately rough.

- **Why it matters (focus, honesty)**: an unordered backlog is a wishlist generating guilt; an ordered one is a strategy generating decisions — "not this sprint" said explicitly beats "maybe someday" said forever. Refinement is where vague wishes become estimable work, which is where planning becomes possible at all.
- **Modern guidance** — order ruthlessly (top 2 sprints refined, the rest rough), estimate relatively (*story points* — complexity vs a reference story, not hours), and delete without mercy (stale items rot into misleading bulk — a backlog longer than 3 months of capacity is an archive, prune it).

### 3) **Velocity measures capacity, not worth**

*Velocity* is completed work per sprint (story points done, averaged over recent sprints) — forecasting fuel: past pace predicts future capacity. It is *not* productivity, quality, or performance — it measures the team's estimating calibration as much as its speed.

- **Why it hurts when misused (gaming, burnout)**: velocity-as-target triggers Goodhart's law instantly — points inflate, tiny tickets multiply, quality collapses, and the number climbs while delivery rots. Comparing velocities across teams is numerology (different scales, domains, realities); using them in performance reviews converts planning data into fear data.
- **Modern guidance** — use velocity only for "how much fits next sprint?" (average of last 3–4, discounted for leave), never for people decisions. Falling velocity is a *diagnostic* (blockers? scope creep? tech debt interest due?) — investigate the system, never interrogate the humans.

### 4) **Common confusion**

- "Agile means no planning" — agile means planning *continuously* in small bets instead of once in a doomed master plan; sprint planning is planning, sharpened.
- "Story points are hours in disguise" — points estimate relative complexity/surprise; the moment they convert to hours, padding and gaming follow — keep them abstract.
- "Carryover means failure" — carryover with a diagnosed cause (underestimated, interrupted, deprioritized) is the system working; silent carryover is the failure.

### 5) **Mentor note**

Teach juniors to read a sprint board like a balance sheet: committed vs done vs carried-with-cause. Anyone who can narrate "we bet 8, delivered 6, the 2 carried because X, so next bet adjusts by Y" understands agile; anyone reciting ceremonies without the numbers is performing ritual — redirect them to the arithmetic until the ritual earns meaning.
