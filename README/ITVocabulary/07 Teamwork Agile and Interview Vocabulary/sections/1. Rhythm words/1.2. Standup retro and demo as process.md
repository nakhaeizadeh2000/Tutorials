## 1.2. Standup, retro, and demo as process

### 1) **Ceremonies serve the cadence**

**Definition** — Agile *ceremonies* are recurring rituals, each protecting one thing: *standup* protects daily alignment, *retro* (retrospective) protects improvement, *demo* (review) protects stakeholder feedback, *planning* protects commitment. A ceremony that protects nothing is a meeting to delete — the cadence is the point, rituals are its servants.

- **What problem it solves**: teams inherit ceremonies as cargo cult — standups because "agile says so", retros nobody acts on, demos nobody attends. The purpose-test ("what does this protect? show me last month's evidence") kills dead ritual and defends live ones against calendar pressure.
- **Modern guidance** — audit quarterly: each ceremony must show artifacts (standup → unblocked items, retro → completed action items, demo → recorded feedback decisions). No artifacts, no ceremony — cancel it and watch whether anything breaks; that experiment teaches more process wisdom than any certification.

```text
# Bad: ritual without protection (cargo-cult agile)
"Daily standup: 12 people, 30 min, status monologues.
 Retro: feelings shared, 0 action items in 3 months.
 Demo: canceled 4 times — 'stakeholders are busy'."

# Good: ceremonies with evidence (each protects something)
"Standup async (bot thread, blockers only). Retro biweekly,
 5/6 last actions completed with owners. Demo monthly —
 last one killed feature X, saving ~3 weeks. Rituals earn kept."
```

### 2) **Retro improves the system**

The retrospective examines the *process*, not the people: what slowed us, what helped, what one change we commit to next sprint — with an owner and a visible tracker. *Blameless* is load-bearing (see [Domain 06, 2.1](<../../../06 Security Cloud and DevOps Vocabulary/sections/2. Flow words/2.1. Pipeline observe and incident words.md>)): blame ends honesty, and dishonest retros improve nothing.

- **Why it matters (compound interest)**: one real improvement per sprint — faster reviews, a flaky test quarantined, a checklist adopted — compounds into a transformed team within quarters. Teams that skip retros don't stay still; they decay, because friction accrues while improvement waits for "someday".
- **Modern guidance** — one action item per retro maximum (completed beats ambitious — five done beats twenty listed), owner + date attached, reviewed first at the next retro. Rotate facilitation so juniors practice process-thinking early; the facilitator learns more than any participant.

### 3) **Demo closes the loop**

The sprint demo puts *working software* in front of stakeholders while change is cheap — feedback arrives when redirection costs days, not after release when it costs quarters. *Stakeholder* here means anyone affected: users, PMs, support, leadership.

- **Why it matters (waste prevention)**: the most expensive bugs aren't code defects but *built-the-wrong-thing* defects — weeks of perfect execution toward misunderstood needs. Demos surface misunderstanding at its cheapest: "oh, we meant the other workflow" in week two is a laugh; in month six it's a rewrite.
- **Modern guidance** — demo working software (never slides — slides demo intentions, software demos reality), invite the people who can say "wrong direction", and record decisions ("stakeholders chose option B — ticket PROJ-88 updated"). A demo without decisions is theater; decisions without demos are gambling.

### 4) **Common confusion**

- "Standup format vs purpose" — format (done/doing/blocked) lives in [Domain 01, 3.2](<../../../01 Core IT Concepts and Professional Jargon/sections/3. Everyday professional jargon/3.2. Meeting jargon standup sync blockers and shipped.md>); purpose (daily alignment inside the sprint bet) lives here — same ceremony, two angles, both linked.
- "Retro is therapy" — feelings surface *data* (frustration points at friction), but the output is systemic action items, not catharsis; feelings without actions are just scheduled complaining.
- "Demo is a status report" — status reports inform; demos *decide* (continue, pivot, kill) — if no decision is possible, skip the demo and send notes.

### 5) **Mentor note**

Let juniors facilitate one retro early — with coaching, not abandonment. Facilitation teaches the meta-skill of this entire domain: extracting signal from groups, converting complaints into owned actions, protecting quiet voices. Nobody learns that from slides; everybody learns it from one well-debriefed facilitation.
