## 5.1. Common interview Q&A: teamwork and career

### 1) **Agile in one paragraph**

*Rhythm + feedback — the interview-safe summary.*

- **Q**: "How does your team work? Describe your agile process."
- **Model answer**: "Two-week sprints with a one-sentence goal; backlog ordered by the PM, refined to two sprints deep; async standups surfacing blockers; PRs reviewed within a day; demo monthly with recorded decisions; retro biweekly producing one owned action item. Velocity forecasts capacity only — never performance. The rhythm that matters: plan, build, demo, improve — every two weeks, with evidence." (from [1.1](<../1. Rhythm words/1.1. Sprint backlog and velocity.md>), [1.2](<../1. Rhythm words/1.2. Standup retro and demo as process.md>))
- **What they are really testing**: whether you've *lived* rhythm or read about it — ceremonies-with-evidence ("5/6 retro actions completed") beats ceremony recitation every time.

### 2) **Conflict and ownership stories**

*STAR answers with DRI clarity.*

- **Q**: "Tell me about a disagreement with a colleague / a time you owned something ambiguous."
- **Model answer** (STAR + DRI): "Situation: PM and I disagreed on API scope two days before sprint end. Task: I was DRI for delivery. Action: 30-min sync with options priced (full scope +4 days vs slice +1), PM chose slice, recorded in the ticket. Result: shipped on time, remainder next sprint — and we added scope-freeze to planning. My line: disagree with data, commit to the decision, record it." (from [2.1](<../2. People words/2.1. Roles stakeholders and ownership.md>))
- **What they are really testing**: disagree-and-commit maturity — principled pushback *plus* full commitment once decided, with the record proving both. Candidates who only escalate or only comply both fail the senior bar.

### 3) **Career questions answered — plus rapid-fire drills**

*Why us, growth, feedback received.*

- **Q**: "Why this role? Where do you see growth? Tell me about feedback you've received."
- **Model answers**: "Why us: your checkout-scale problems match the ambiguous-migration scope I'm targeting for senior — plus blameless postmortems, which is the culture I do my best work in. Growth: leading end-to-end (gap) via owning the auth migration (vehicle) by Q1 (date). Feedback: my manager noted thin incident write-ups (behavior) slowing handovers (impact) — I adopted the timeline template, and the last two handovers needed zero follow-ups (change evidenced)." (from [2.2](<../2. People words/2.2. Feedback ladder and one-on-ones.md>))
- **Rapid-fire drills** (one line each):
  - *Sprint?* — "Fixed-length bet with a one-sentence goal — date never moves, scope flexes."
  - *Velocity?* — "Past pace forecasting future capacity — planning fuel, never a performance score."
  - *Retro?* — "Process examination producing one owned improvement — the sprint's compound interest."
  - *Stakeholder?* — "Anyone with skin in the outcome — mapped early, informed proportionally."
  - *DRI?* — "One named human ensuring resolution — delegate or escalate, but guarantee motion."
  - *1:1?* — "The report's meeting for career and blockers — agenda from below, outcomes in writing."

### 4) **Common confusion**

- "Behavioral questions want modesty" — they want *owned impact* with evidence; "we" without your "I" (what *you* decided, priced, recorded) reads as passenger, not driver.
- "Career answers should be ambitious-vague" ("I want to grow!") — level + gap + vehicle + date outscores ambition theater by miles.
- "Feedback stories show weakness" — they show *coachability with receipts* — the trait interviewers actually hire for, since skills can be taught and defensiveness cannot.

### 5) **Mentor note**

Rehearse stories, not answers: two conflict stories, two ownership stories, one feedback story — each STAR-shaped with numbers and records attached. Candidates who story-bank interview like storytellers (concrete, paced, honest about their line); candidates who memorize lines interview like examinees. Run one mock where you interrupt mid-story — recovery poise is what panels remember.
