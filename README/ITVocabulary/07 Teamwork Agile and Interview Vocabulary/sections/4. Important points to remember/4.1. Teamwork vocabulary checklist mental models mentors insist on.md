## 4.1. Teamwork vocabulary checklist (mental models mentors insist on)

### 1) **Name the cadence**

Sprint length, ceremony purpose, artifacts produced — in every process complaint (from [1.1](<../1. Rhythm words/1.1. Sprint backlog and velocity.md>), [1.2](<../1. Rhythm words/1.2. Standup retro and demo as process.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "2-week sprints, retro actions 5/6 completed, demo monthly with recorded decisions" — rhythm with evidence. Process complaints without these nouns ("agile isn't working") are moods, not diagnoses.
- **Bad habit to break**: defending or attacking ceremonies by faith — audit by artifacts, keep what protects something, cancel the rest.

### 2) **Name the owner**

DRI per problem — before any escalation, status, or complaint (from [2.1](<../2. People words/2.1. Roles stakeholders and ownership.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "flake-rate: DRI is Sam, fix by Friday" — one neck, one date. Every ownerless problem in your standup is a decision you're postponing, not a mystery you're enduring.
- **Bad habit to break**: "someone should…" — the most expensive two words in teamwork; convert to a name before the sentence ends.

### 3) **Name the growth ask**

Level, scope gap, timeline — in every career conversation (from [2.2](<../2. People words/2.2. Feedback ladder and one-on-ones.md>)).

- **Check yourself**: "targeting senior: gap is ambiguous-problem leadership — leading the auth migration end-to-end by Q1" — level, gap, vehicle, date. Vague growth wishes ("I want to grow") get vague support; specified asks get sponsorship.
- **Bad habit to break**: annual career thinking — growth compounds weekly (brag document, feedback sought, scope stretched); yearly reviews merely recognize what weekly habits built.

### 4) **Common confusion**

- "Process words are soft skills" — cadence, ownership, and growth language move delivery dates and retention numbers; "soft" here means load-bearing-but-invisible, like foundations.
- "Checklists don't apply to people" — the checklist constrains *your* clarity (name cadence/owner/ask), not their behavior — precision about people respects them more than vagueness ever could.

### 5) **Mentor note**

Open every team health check with the triple-name round: cadence evidence, ownerless-problem scan, growth-ask review. Fifteen minutes, monthly — teams that speak these nouns fluently self-correct between check-ins, which is the entire difference between managed teams and led ones.
