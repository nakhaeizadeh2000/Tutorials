## 5.1. Common interview Q&A: data and AI vocabulary

### 1) **SQL vs NoSQL**

*The shape-choice answer with a trade-off sentence.*

- **Q**: "When would you choose SQL vs NoSQL?"
- **Model answer**: "Relational by default — my data almost certainly relates, and I want joins, constraints, and consistency. Document or key-value stores when records genuinely change shape or access is key-shaped at scale. The choice follows the access pattern, not fashion — and I'd name the queries first, because the shape question decides the store." (from [1.2](<../1. Data words/1.2. Database table query and index.md>))
- **What they are really testing**: whether you've been burned by fashion-driven choices — "we migrated back" stories with named queries outscore theoretical comparisons every time.

### 2) **What is a token**

*Billing atom + context window — the two implications.*

- **Q**: "What is a token in the LLM context?"
- **Model answer**: "A word-piece — the unit models read and write. Two implications: cost (bills count tokens, so long contexts are expensive) and design (the context window caps input+output, so relevance beats volume — retrieve the right slice instead of dumping history)." (from [2.1](<../2. AI words/2.1. Model token and prompt.md>))
- **What they are really testing**: cost-awareness — engineers who mention the billing atom unprompted have shipped AI features; those who don't have demoed them.

### 3) **Training vs inference — plus rapid-fire drills**

*Past learning vs present answering.*

- **Q**: "Explain training vs inference. How do you handle hallucinations?"
- **Model answer**: "Training adjusts weights once, expensively; inference runs the frozen model per request, cheaply — so I climb the cheapest rung first: prompt, then ground with retrieval, then fine-tune, and treat train-from-scratch as essentially never. Hallucinations are permanent base behavior, so I design around them: constrain outputs to verifiable forms, verify before acting, and eval everything on fixed sample sets." (from [2.2](<../2. AI words/2.2. Training vs inference and hallucination.md>))
- **Rapid-fire drills** (one line each):
  - *Index?* — "Lookup structure trading write cost for read speed — propose with both sides quoted."
  - *Prompt?* — "Prose that steers a model — versioned, reviewed, and evaluated like code."
  - *Hallucination?* — "Fluent fabrication — design the system for what happens when it occurs, not if."
  - *Structured vs unstructured?* — "Rows-and-schemas vs free-form — the split that decides store and tooling."
  - *Dataset vs label?* — "The collection vs its answer-keys — quality of the second dominates the first."
  - *RAG?* — "Grounding answers in retrieved docs — cited lookup instead of fluent guessing."

### 4) **Common confusion**

- "AI questions want math" — vocabulary-level interviews want price tags, failure modes, and design responses; gradients come later, in specialist loops.
- "Saying 'it depends' looks evasive" — "it depends on the access pattern — here are the two branches" is the strongest sentence in data interviews.
- "Token math is trivia" — token budgets decide feature viability; interviewers asking it are checking whether you've ever seen the invoice.

### 5) **Mentor note**

Grade mock answers on *price tags and failure paths*: did SQL-vs-NoSQL name the queries? Did tokens mention cost? Did hallucinations get a system response, not a hope? Candidates who attach economics and failure-walks to vocabulary prove production experience — drill exactly that attachment.
